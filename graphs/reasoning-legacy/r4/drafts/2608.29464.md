Cluster block: L. Chain-of-thought faithfulness

Title: Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness of Reasoning Models Varies with Where and How Preference Cues Are Delivered

The row as drafted from the abstract (`source` `abstract`):

- `venue+year`: arXiv 2026-08
- `method name`: FACE-Eval
- `method summary`: FAITHFULNESS (with a secondary monitor-detection measure). The cue-injection setup varies cue *location* (user message vs tool return, i.e. a prefilled tool result) and *explicitness* (direct summary vs raw artifact), measuring verbalised commitment among cue-following answers and unverbalised adoption (the cue shifted the answer without being mentioned) among all cued samples; single-call, prefilled-tool setting only. Separately, two transcript monitors are asked to detect preference adoption from the transcript.
- `datasets / models`: FACE-Eval, 5,100 samples; 15 open-weight models from eight families, 4B–1.60T params; GPT-5.6-Luna and GPT-4o-mini as monitors
- `metric`: verbalised commitment; unverbalised adoption; monitor detection (Pearson correlation)
- `result`: Every model shows lower verbalised commitment for tool-return than user-message cues, and for implicit than explicit cues, than the user-message/explicit baseline. Unverbalised adoption is higher for tool-return cues on all 15 models and for implicit cues in 28 of 30 model-channel comparisons. Telling models their reasoning will be monitored does not reliably close the gap. Across 32 model-channel-explicitness cells, higher unverbalised adoption correlates with lower monitor detection ability for both monitors (Pearson r = −0.54 and −0.78) — no snippet coverage beyond the abstract text quoted here, so this is unverified against methods/results text
- `evidence`: empirical
- `measures`: faithfulness
- `elicitation`: elicited
- `pressure`: prompt-level
