## The Interplay of Data Structure and Imbalance in the Learning Dynamics of Diffusion Models

## Method

- L42-97 (Problem Formulation, Section 2): Score-based diffusion model setup with forward and backward SDEs (Eq. 1-2); score matching loss (Eq. 3) trained on random-feature model with tanh activation
- L97-148 (Problem Formulation continued): "We analyze these training regimes using Random Matrix Theory to characterize the spectrum of U in the high-dimensional proportional limit where N,P,M→∞ with fixed O(1) ratios χp=P/N and χm=M/N" — high-dimensional analysis framework
- L148-175 (Gaussian Equivalence Procedure): Uses conditional Gaussian equivalence for random feature models in mixture setting to derive asymptotically equivalent formulation (Eq. 9)
- L180-250 (Section 3 main results): Three-step characterization: (1) class-specific speciation times in backward diffusion; (2) global timescale separation between generalization and memorization; (3) class-specific learning trajectories
- L240-293 (Result 2 and 3): "Class variance is the primary determinant"; "centroid geometry plays a secondary role"; "Sampling imbalance acts as a modulator" — hierarchy of structural effects
- L436-710 (Appendices C-F): Gradient flow equations, Gaussian equivalence derivations, spectral equations of U using replica method and random matrix theory

## Setup

- L52-70 (Data Distribution): Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with C components (Eq. 5); class centroids m_c, conditional variances σ_c², mixing proportions b_c with ||m_c||=O(√N) and σ_c=O(1)
- L70-86 (Training Dynamics): Random feature model with fixed random projection W and learnable readout weights A; exact gradient flow solution provided (Eq. 6)
- L326-387 (Section 4 Experiments): Fashion MNIST dataset with binary class pairs, Sneaker paired with nine other classes at three Sneaker proportions (25%, 50%, 75%) on 8,000 images with 32 log-spaced checkpoints; U-Net backbone for DDPM

## Headline results

- L159-175 (Result 1, Speciation Times): "Weak Imbalance: When all classes are proportionally represented (b_c=O(1)), all classes speciate simultaneously at t_s=(1/2)log N+O(1)" and "Strong Imbalance: When a class is severely undersampled (b_c=O(N^{-a_c}) for 0<a_c<1), the system exhibits a hierarchy of class-dependent speciation times: t_s^(c)=(1-a_c)/2 log N+O(1)" — speciation time hierarchy
- L240-262 (Result 2, Hierarchy of Learning): High-variance classes are learned first, followed by smaller-norm centroid classes; sampling imbalance can reverse this ordering
- L297-303 (Result 3, Generalization-Memorization Trade-off): "Undersampling the high-variance class forces the model to generalize the low-variance class first" and "Oversampling the high-variance class deprives the low-variance class of data, causing it to be memorized first" — fundamental trade-off
- L340-386 (Result 4, RF/DDPM Consistency): "Using only pair-level statistics [class variances, centroid norms, and cosine similarities], the RF model evaluated on Gaussian synthetic data qualitatively matches the memorization-gap trends of the full DDPM"

## Limitations

- L393-403 (Section 5 Conclusions): "Our analysis is limited in two main respects. First, our presentation focused on binary Gaussian mixtures with isotropic covariances, even though the framework naturally extends to multi-class and non-isotropic settings. Second, it is fundamentally restricted to capturing first- and second-order structures."
- L398-403: "This leaves open the question of how more complex heterogeneities affect generative performance"; "dataset outliers may act as strong attractors"; "real data classes often have internal multimodal structure that cannot be reduced to a single Gaussian component"

## Assumptions and conditions

- L52-70 (Data model assumptions): "We assume the training dataset D=\{x_ν\}_ν=1^M is sampled from a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with C components"; assumes class variances are homogeneous within class (isotropic Σ_c=σ_c²I_N)
- L148-175 (High-dimensional regime): Results require N,P,M→∞ with fixed O(1) ratios; empirical validation on N∈{50,500,5000}, P∈{100,1000,10000}, M=2000
- L211-218 (Small diffusion time regime): Analysis focuses on "small diffusion times (t≪1)" and "large-time regime (small e^{-t})"
- L318-324 (Evaluation metric): Memorization assessed using distance ratio criterion: "a generated sample x_c is considered memorized if the distance ratio to its nearest training neighbors satisfies ||x_c-nn₁(x_c)||_2/||x_c-nn₂(x_c)||_2<1/3"

## Dimensions

- L42-148 (evidence): `derived` — paper develops "a high-dimensional analytical framework" using Random Matrix Theory; results are limit arguments and asymptotic derivations, not proofs of general finite-case or empirical measurements
- L42-52 (measures): `monitorability` — the paper studies how "different sub-populations may be learned at different stages of training" and measures this through class-specific learning time observation; directly testing whether monitor reading training traces can detect per-class disparities
- L326-387 (elicitation): Both `observational` (Fashion MNIST DDPM trained naturally) and `spontaneous` (memorization arises from standard score matching without explicit per-class training)
- L52-70, 318-324 (pressure): `none` — the analysis is observational; no adversarial pressure, training reweighting, or constraint-based testing applied; just characterizing what happens under standard score matching loss
