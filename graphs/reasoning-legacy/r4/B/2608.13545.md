- `method name`: confirmed — LittleCurriculum (corpus) / LittleLearner (model) — "we introduce LittleCurriculum , a curated 88B-token pretraining corpus tailored to U.S. elementary school material" (L10)
- `method summary`: corrected — Filters FineWeb-Edu into K–5 and Beyond-K–5 subsets with a multi-stage pipeline: an Age-of-Acquisition pre-filter, LLM-as-judge annotation (CCSS-based prompts) used to train FastText and then ModernBERT grade classifiers, a regex symbolic filter, and frequency-based removal of Beyond-K–5 terms. The design is deliberately precision-first: about 0% Beyond-K–5 retention on the ground-truth set, at the cost of keeping only about 35% of K–5 text. A 5B Qwen3-architecture model is then trained from scratch (100 h on 8 B200s), compared with a same-size model trained on unfiltered FineWeb-Edu and with Gemma 2B. The sandbox is used to test whether scaling, SFT+GRPO post-training, and in-context learning extend capability beyond the exposure boundary. — "We divide the web corpus FineWeb-Edu [1] into an elementary school (K–5) and Beyond-K–5 subset, via multi-stage filtering" (L76); "The pipeline successfully reduces the retention of Beyond-K–5 content to 0% while preserving ~35% of K–5" (L102); "We follow the Qwen3 [42] architecture and pretrain the model for 100 hours on 8 NVIDIA B200 GPUs." ## Limitations and conditions

- "we acknowledge that certain emergent behaviors like in-context learning may be less pronounced than at frontier scales" (L230) — the ICL negative result is at 5B scale.
- "We do not find that for the 5B LittleLearner and the few shot and explanation setups we test" (L194) — the ICL result is limited to the tested prompting setups.
- "Crucially, within the tested post-training budgets, we observe no difference" (L180) — the post-training conclusion is conditional on budget.
- "in our tested settings" (L204) — the capability-ceiling claim is scoped by the authors to the tested interventions.
- "This trend partly reflects the limitations of grade-level CCSS labels as a proxy for problem difficulty for language models." (L156) — grade labels are an imperfect difficulty axis.
- "LittleLearner should not be read as a digital 12-year-old" (L444) — skill acquisition order does not follow the human curriculum.
- "The similar performance on Grade 6 is not a failure of our filtering pipeline, but rather a mismatch between the human-aligned CCSS and the evaluation" (L442) — Grade 6 does not separate the two models.
- "This low K–5 retention reflects our deliberate precision-over-recall design" (L102) — the corpus omits about 65% of genuine K–5 material, and removed passages are longer and lexically more complex.
- "The filter retains 2.48% of 6,000 Beyond-K–5 passages" (L104) — on the external WeeBit set the boundary is not perfectly clean (0.05% genuinely out-of-scope on manual check; 0.09% n-gram matches corpus-wide).
- "on out-of-scope prompts, the model does not express uncertainty but instead generates structured yet incorrect explanations" (L564) — out-of-scope failures are confident confabulations.

## Other findings

- The draft says post-training does "not raise" out-of-scope capability. The body says post-training "only modestly improves" Beyond-K–5 performance, so the gain is small but not zero.
- The "post-training" experiments are SFT+GRPO on math (MathCAMPS), not general knowledge injection; ICL is few-shot hand-written CoT and explanations.
- The smallest LittleLearner (0.6B) outperforms the Unfiltered control in-scope (L176), a specialization advantage that disappears at larger sizes.
- The pass@k analysis is the authors' argument that the ceiling is not a sampling artifact: curves flatten by k≈100 (L462).
