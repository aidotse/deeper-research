# Map: 2609.13997 — Unlocking the Unsolvable: Teacher-Guided Curriculum for Data-Efficient RLVR

## Method

- L4-5 (title and approach): "Unlocking the Unsolvable: Teacher-Guided Curriculum for Data-Efficient RLVR... partial reasoning traces from a stronger model create a graded difficulty landscape, and a backward-chaining curriculum progressively withdraws guidance" — method overview
- L27 (core insight): "a partial reasoning trace from a stronger teacher transforms a single unsolvable problem into a family of states with graded difficulty, indexed by a guidance level ρ∈[0,1] controlling how much of the teacher's reasoning is revealed" — guidance mechanism
- L71-77 (guidance implementation): "Letting n_steps(s) denote the number of steps in T(s), we expose the first g steps: g = round(ρ·n_steps(s)), s_ρ = s ∪ {first g steps of T(s)}. The student receives the augmented prompt s_ρ in which the teacher's partial trace is presented as auxiliary context" — how guidance is applied
- L89 (backward-chaining paradigm): "begin at high ρ where the student reliably succeeds, and progressively withdraw guidance toward ρ=0, where the student must solve the problem entirely on its own" — curriculum strategy
- L95 (backward-chaining definition): "A curriculum is backward-chaining if it places non-trivial training mass at ρ>0 and progressively shifts that mass toward ρ=0" — formal definition
- L101-103 (Mixture curriculum): "A mixture curriculum bypasses the scheduler by expanding the per-sample state space: each problem is replicated at M discretized ρ-levels... expanding the 128 unsolvable problems into a 640-row dataset trained with standard GRPO" — baseline 1
- L107 (AdaBack curriculum): "Per sample s, AdaBack maintains an integer interval [g_min(s),g_max(s)]... AdaBack then updates the interval bidirectionally: a success (r̄≥τ) sets g_max←g_used and resets g_min←0; a failure (r̄<τ) raises g_min←g_used" — baseline 2
- L135-156 (MFC design): "MFC implements this goal by lowering a sample's guidance frontier only after the student succeeds with less guidance... success commits, failure is forgotten. AdaBack raises g_min on failure. MFC keeps only the success-side ratchet, committing g_curr←g_used on a successful visit if smaller" — proposed method

## Setup

- L59 (model and budget): "We use n=8 rollouts per group and set N=64: a problem with no success in 64 attempts is overwhelmingly likely to produce n=8 failures at almost every training step" — unsolvable definition and rollout budget
- L59 (corpus size): "From our 2,000-problem competition-mathematics corpus approximately 7% of problems (147 total) are 64-unsolvable for Qwen3-1.7B" — unsolvable problem frequency
- L71 (teacher model): "We employ DeepSeek-V3.2 DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025b) as the teacher model throughout this work" — teacher selection
- L195 (base models): "We use Qwen3-1.7B Yang et al. (2025) as the primary base model and Qwen3-0.6B as a smaller-scale replicate" — student models
- L197-200 (datasets): "All training and in-domain evaluation data are subsets of OpenR1-Math-220k... 2,000-problem mixed-difficulty training set (OR1-2k) and a disjoint 200-problem held-out test set (OR1-200)" — training corpora
- L203 (GRPO core): "all four share an identical GRPO core (batch size 128, n=8 rollouts at temperature 1, 1e-6 learning rate, low-variance KL with coefficient 1e-3, 8,192-token response cap)" — standardized RLVR setup
- L207 (evaluation benchmarks): "Evaluation spans nine benchmarks in three groups: two in-domain math sets (OR1-200 and Uns-22), five out-of-distribution math sets (MATH-500, AMC23, AIME24/25/26), and two out-of-distribution non-math reasoning sets (SciBench, GPQA-Diamond)" — benchmark selection

## Headline results

- L11 (data efficiency): "Training on only 128 unsolvable problems matches or exceeds GRPO trained on a full 2,000-problem corpus (~16× data efficiency) on the nine-benchmark average for both base models" — main efficiency claim
- L11 (frontier expansion): "substantially expanding the reasoning boundary measured by pass@k at large k" — coverage improvement
- L29 (training matching or exceeding): "Training on 128 unsolvable problems with backward-chaining curricula matches or exceeds GRPO on the full 2,000-problem corpus (~16× data reduction) on the nine-benchmark average" — headline result
- L29 (coverage expansion): "substantially expands the reasoning boundary (AIME pass@k improves at every k up to 256)" — pass@k improvements
- L171-177 (Table 2 results on 1.7B): "Qwen3-1.7B: MFC 68.2 OR1-200, 31.8 Uns-22, 47.3 average vs GRPO 68.6 OR1-200, 23.9 Uns-22, 44.3 average" — pass@1 scores for main model
- L179-187 (Table 2 results on 0.6B): "Qwen3-0.6B: MFC 50.4 OR1-200, 23.3 Uns-22, 34.0 average vs GRPO 51.1 OR1-200, 16.5 Uns-22, 31.3 average" — pass@1 scores for smaller model
- L220-221 (MFC cross-benchmark): "MFC has the best cross-benchmark average on both base models. Its averages are 47.3 and 34.0. On the AIME24/25/26 average it improves over GRPO by +4.7/+3.7 pp on Qwen3-1.7B/0.6B" — best method comparison
- L233 (pass@256 gains): "At pass@256, it reaches 70.0% on Qwen3-1.7B and 53.9% on Qwen3-0.6B, exceeding GRPO by +10.0 pp for both models" — large-k frontier expansion
- L79 (unlock curve): "Table 1 shows the unlock curve: pass@8 as a function of ρ on base Qwen3-1.7B over the 147 unsolvable problems... pass@8 increases from 2.2% at ρ=0 to 34.0% at ρ=0.25 and 85.4% at ρ=1" — validation that guidance unlocks unsolvable problems

## Limitations

- L289 (teacher dependency): "Our work relies on a single fixed teacher model (DeepSeek-V3.2) to generate all reasoning traces, and the quality of the resulting curriculum is inevitably tied to the quality of these traces" — teacher model choice matters
- L289 (teacher interaction): "We do not study how teacher capability interacts with curriculum design" — unexplored parameter space
- L291 (domain specificity): "The framework is trained only on mathematical reasoning with single-turn LLM outputs" — single domain
- L291 (transfer boundaries): "Transfer to code generation or formal theorem proving is untested" — other domains not explored
- L291 (agentic tasks): "We have not applied the curriculum to agentic tasks, which need sequential environment actions and multi-step tool-use traces" — action-sequential domains excluded

## Assumptions and conditions

- L57-58 (N-unsolvability definition): "For a policy π₀ and a sampling budget N, a problem s is N-unsolvable if pass@N(π₀,s)=0, i.e., no correct solution is found among N independent rollouts" — definition of what makes a problem unsolvable
- L59 (filtering logic): "The budget N should be large enough relative to the GRPO per-prompt rollout count n so that N-unsolvability reliably predicts uniform failure within each training group" — why N=64 with n=8
- L67 (waste hypothesis): "under GRPO, the vast majority of unsolvable problems are structurally excluded from learning throughout training" — empirical finding that unsolvable problems are wasted under standard training
- L113-121 (distribution shift): "Whenever g(s,ρ)>0, the curriculum induces a distribution shift between training and test prompts. Improving J_C^(t) does not automatically improve J_0: augmented visits transfer to the unguided regime through parameter sharing, but the transfer is sign-indeterminate in general" — formal condition identifying distribution-shift cost
- L123-127 (unguided training mass): "Higher M₀^(t) means more compute on prompts matching the test distribution... This surrogate matters more in the unsolvable-only regime than in mixed-difficulty training" — why unguided training mass matters for unsolvable problems
- L127 (regime specificity): "In an unsolvable-only corpus, samples produce near-zero feasibility at ρ=0 initially, and none of the existing curricula carry an explicit mechanism to drive descent toward ρ=0 as the policy improves" — condition under which MFC is necessary

## Dimensions

- **Evidence**: L11 (empirical demonstration): "Training on only 128 unsolvable problems matches or exceeds GRPO trained on a full 2,000-problem corpus (~16× data efficiency)... We further show that this paradigm expands the reasoning boundary (pass@k gains at every k up to 256)" — empirical results on multiple benchmarks and model scales

- **Measures**: L19 (frontier expansion as measure): "the reasoning boundary tends to plateau or even shrink after standard training... What, then, about problems operationally beyond that boundary?" — measures whether models can reach new problems (pass@k at large k), not just solve problems faster

- **Elicitation**: L70-71 (teacher-guided unsolvable problems): "we employ DeepSeek-V3.2 as the teacher model... for each unsolvable problem s, we prompt the teacher model to generate a structured reasoning trace T(s)" — curriculum method deliberately elicits reasoning from a stronger model and uses it to guide training; guidance level ρ is actively controlled during training

- **Pressure**: L115-119 (test-time objective formalization): "The training objective at step t under curriculum C samples (s,ρ)∼C^(t)... The test-time objective is the unguided case J₀(θ)=E_{s∼D}... Whenever g(s,ρ)>0, the curriculum induces a distribution shift" — training under intermediate guidance levels ρ>0 is systematically different from test distribution (ρ=0); no adversarial pressure but curriculum creates deliberate train-test mismatch that MFC aims to minimize
