- `method summary`: corrected — Four production harnesses (Aider, OpenHands, Qwen Code, SWE-agent) collect a frozen trajectory corpus on a shared SWE-Gym pool, with training on 183 tasks (L123) and 81,216 updates per arm (L107); the map's record and episode counts were not confirmed in the text read. From one Qwen3-8B SFT warm start, the Within and Cross arms replay identical records, tokens, rewards, loss masks and update budget, differing only in the GRPO normalisation boundary. Further arms: coverage-matched and residualised (change what enters the pooled group); two extra training seeds for Within and Cross; one Cross arm that re-collects its second half on-policy. Checkpoints are graded by a sealed SWE-bench Verified oracle on the four source harnesses and a held-out minimal "weak-ReAct" harness. An out-of-fold classifier measures how much harness identity each arm's advantage carries, and action-label distributions per harness are compared by Jensen-Shannon divergence. ## Limitations and conditions

- "Every estimate other than the Within and Cross held-out levels rests on one checkpoint, and the source harnesses were evaluated at two attempts per task." (L149) — most numbers are single-checkpoint.
- "Absolute solve rates are low ( $1$ – $10\%$ ) and the endpoint is binary." (L149) — the floor effect limits what any recipe can show.
- "Training covers one model family (Qwen Team, 2025b; Hui et al., 2024), one budget, four source harnesses, one round of on-policy re-collection, and Python-only tasks." (L149)
- "The second-model comparison uses one untrained checkpoint per model, under context budgets that differ between the two families" (L149) — the cross-model harness-strength claim is weakly supported.
- "The design resolves differences of about $1\,\mathrm{pp}$ and larger." (L149) — the null is a "no difference of about 1 pp or more", not an equivalence.
- "The six held-out contrasts were fixed before the checkpoints existed and not registered externally" (L149)
- "Multilingual, test-generation and contamination-hardened variants of the benchmark" (L149) — outside the study.
- "Systems that report multi-harness gains also use larger task pools (Yang et al., 2025), train longer (Liu et al., 2025a), combine supervision stages" (L137) — the null applies only to the grouping rule with exposure fixed, not to multi-harness RL as practised.
- "The single-seed estimate sits at the top of its own seed distribution." (L105) — the k=8 +0.25 pp headline is a high draw.
- "As assembled, it removed 9% of that offset." (L123) — the residualised arm failed to remove the harness offset, so the paper has no working "harness-identity removed" condition.
- "A spot check of the labeller on $160$ turns found $11$ mislabelled, $8$ of them under SWE-agent." (L127) — the action-distribution result relies on an imperfect labeller.
- "Under Cross, then, rewards from contexts that are not exchangeable in the usual same-prompt GRPO sense compete for credit." (L91) — the premise motivating the comparison.

## Other findings

- The held-out finding is stronger and more negative than the draft conveys. The six recipes span only 4.01% to 4.73% on the held-out harness, below the smallest detectable difference. No RL recipe, and not even SFT, beats the untrained base there (L95). RL training in this setup produces no detectable portable gain at all, whichever the grouping rule.
- For cluster K, the paper's own framing is "configuration adaptation without portable capability" (L133): gains appear on the source harnesses, largest on OpenHands for both arms (L99-101).
- The training corpus is small: 183 tasks, selected as those on which harnesses disagree (L123; manifest size per the map at L82, consistent with L107's 81,216 updates).
