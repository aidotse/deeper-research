## Cells

- `method name`: corrected — transition-level audit against a measured null (the paper's own terms. "Measured-null transition auditing" is a close paraphrase, but the paper names no single method.) — "Transition-level auditing therefore requires a separately measured null for every statistic it reports" (Abstract, l.11)
- `method summary`: corrected — Audits per-problem transition ledgers. Solve rates are estimated from k = 128 samples and turned into solved/unsolved states with a hysteresis band of [0.41, 0.59]. Each problem then falls into one of seven ledger categories, and the corruption-to-learning ratio (CLR) is read off. The expansion statistic ER_m is also computed. Every statistic is read against a frozen copy of the base model pushed through the identical pipeline. Thresholded expansion is replaced by a one-sided Fisher exact test per problem against a baseline pooled over the 11 independent checkpoint-0 evaluations, under FDR control. Arms are compared on a ladder matched in stream, retained volume and evaluation, so only the correctness filter varies. — "We measure how many by re-evaluating a frozen model at every checkpoint and computing every statistic exactly as for a trained one; each transition it reports is an artifact by construction." (l.81); "Then test each problem’s endpoint count against it with a one-sided Fisher exact test under FDR control." (l.125)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — Qwen3-8B with rank-32 LoRA, three rounds of 256 problems from one stream. Arms: STaR, majority-vote SFT (TTRL-like), a policy-gradient arm using the vote as reward, and distillation from gpt-oss-120b as a positive control, with three seeds on the matched ladder. Evaluated on MATH-500 (200-problem subsample), AIME 2025 and 2026 (60 problems), and a constructed difficulty band of 1,163 MATH problems, at k = 128, T = 0.8, top-p 0.95, plus one greedy sample. — "All arms share the backbone (Qwen3-8B, Yang et al., 2025), rank-32 LoRA, three rounds of $256$ problems from one stream, and evaluation at $k{=}128$" (l.87)
- `metric`: corrected — Per-problem ledger counts (learned or corrupted) and CLR against a frozen-model floor. The expansion rate ER_m against its null across 110 frozen pairs. Per-problem exact-test detections against a pooled 1,408-draw baseline, stratified by base rate. — "The headline quantity is the corruption-to-learning ratio" (l.53); "Detections are problems whose solve rate rises significantly against a pooled $1{,}408$ -draw baseline of the untrained model" (Table 2 caption, l.151)
- `result`: confirmed, with qualifiers the draft omits — Seven measurement failures, each inverting a reported finding when uncontrolled. On the untrained model, a single greedy decode gives 6 apparent learnings and 9 corruptions (CLR 1.5), and ER_1 = 0.280, i.e. 7 of 25 AIME problems "expanded" by a frozen model. The m = 2 repair's null is 0.058 [0.038, 0.078] over 110 frozen pairs, not zero. On the 22 AIME problems reached at most 5/1,408 times, distillation detects 8–11 per seed against 0–2 for three self-training arms. A logistic model rejects "larger overall gain" as the explanation (β = 1.91, p < 10⁻⁸). On the 10 never-reached problems the difference is not significant (5 vs 2, p = 0.35), so the claim concerns rarely-reached problems, not expansion. STaR and majority-vote SFT corrupt 106 and 88 of 1,163 band problems against a design-matched floor of 8. The draft's remark about snippet search should be dropped. — "Distillation improves $8$ – $11$ of the $22$ problems the base model reaches at most five times in $1{,}408$ draws, against $0$ – $2$ for three forms of self-training, and a logistic model rejects the reading that this merely follows from the teacher’s larger overall gain ( $\beta=1.91$ , $p<10^{-8}$ )" (l.27); "on the ten problems the base model never reaches at all the comparison is not significant, so the claim is about rarely-reached problems and not about expansion" (l.27)

## Limitations and conditions

- "Three rounds and roughly $270$ optimizer steps of rank- $32$ LoRA, far short of the schedules we position against: what we establish is that this regime does not expand, not that self-training cannot." (Limitations, l.201) — scope of the negative result on self-training.
- "Expansion rests on $22$ low-base problems on one benchmark, and on the $10$ the base model never reaches the comparison is not significant." (l.201) — the expansion claim is small-sample and inconclusive at base rate zero.
- "Corruption rests on a band drawn from MATH training problems almost certainly in the backbone’s pretraining data and built to make corruption observable." (l.201) — the corruption counts are not a natural forgetting rate. Also: "its counts are therefore not a forgetting rate for a natural distribution" (l.85).
- "One backbone family carries every claim (Appendix E.3)." (l.201) — Qwen3-8B only.
- "no successes in $128$ draws bounds the base pass@1 probability at $q\leq 0.023$ with $95\%$ confidence, it does not establish $q=0$" (l.77) — "base-unreached" is relative to a sampling budget, not to the support of the distribution.
- "Neither is the comparison on the ten low-base problems of the uncontaminated $2026$ half ( $6$ against $2$ , $p=0.17$ )." (l.127) — on the uncontaminated half alone, the dissociation is not significant.
- "the arm labeled majority-vote SFT below is cross-entropy fine-tuning on the rationales that agree with the vote" (l.89) — a deviation from TTRL as published. TTRL's native on-set configuration is reported only as an unmatched baseline.
- "a majority-vote method’s outcome is not determined by its design alone" (F5, l.139) — majority-vote self-training lands on either side of CLR = 1 across seeds (0.55, 1.53, 1.33).
- "under a pure endpoint rule the two self-training methods coincide exactly and at $z=0$ their order reverses, so we rest nothing on the between-method comparison" (l.191) — STaR vs majority-vote ordering is not claimed.
- "so we report a bound (any change here is smaller than about eleven points) rather than a null result" (l.191) — the refusal/safety probe is inconclusive.
- "only at $G=9$ does it hold within $\pm 0.02$ of the value eleven give" (F7, l.143) — measured nulls need about nine or more baseline replicates; four is not enough.
- "We report roughly a dozen $p$ -values across seven failure modes and correct within each family rather than across them" (l.197) — the multiple-comparison correction is within-family only.
- "The comparison is in any case conservative in the teacher’s disfavor, since the distillation arms truncate $36$ – $46\%$ of completions even at an $8{,}192$ -token cap" (l.129) — distillation's numbers are depressed by truncation, and its corruption counts are excluded (F3, l.135).
- "This does not bias the transition counts, for a specific reason: selection uses an independent $k{=}8$ profile" (App. B.1, l.385) — the assumption that answers the regression-to-the-mean objection on the band. The cost is reduced power.

## Other findings

- Self-training does sometimes lift rarely-reached problems: "self-training does occasionally raise a rarely-reached problem above the noise, at roughly a fifth of the teacher’s rate" (l.129). The draft's "three forms of self-training do not" should be read as 0–2 detections, not zero.
- Corruption coexists with accuracy gains in the same run. STaR destroys 106 band problems while its mean accuracy rises 5.6 points (l.187), and more than half of the corruptions exceed any solve-rate movement the frozen model produced across 20 pairs.
- The paper is a measurement and methodology contribution, which bears on block J. Its substantive finding, that self-training in this regime sharpens rather than expands, is presented as aligning with the sharpening account (l.27). It is not a model-collapse result about recursive synthetic data.
- The 0.280 figure comes from a single frozen comparison on 25 AIME problems, so it is itself a one-shot estimate, which the paper's own F7 point makes.
