# Map for 2603.12163: A Quantitative Characterization of Forgetting in Post-Training

## Method

- L15-19 (Introduction): "Can we precisely quantify when a post-training procedure induces forgetting and when it does not?" — Core research question about post-training forgetting mechanisms
- L21-29 (Setup): "We define a true target mixture $p_\alpha(y) = \alpha p_o(y) + (1-\alpha)p_n(y)$" — Two-mode mixture model abstracting continual learning into old and new distributions
- L31-40 (Forgetting types): "Mass forgetting corresponds to the collapse of mixture mass on the old mode, whereas old-component drift occurs when the model retains nonzero mass on the old mode but its parameters move away from the true old distribution" — Defines two forms of forgetting
- L43-46 (Divergence contrast): "The forward-KL is the population analogue of maximum likelihood on a data distribution" and "The reverse-KL objective, is the population analogue of matching the model to a target distribution under on-policy sampling" — Contrasts forward vs reverse KL objectives
- L117-118 (Disjoint-support intuition): "This provides a clean caricature of catastrophic forgetting as mass collapse driven by off-policy training" — Simplified case for building intuition

## Setup

- L49-50 (Gaussian model): "We consider the case where $p_o = \mathcal{N}(\mu_o, \Sigma)$ and $p_n = \mathcal{N}(\mu_n, \Sigma)$ with separation $\delta := \|\mu_n - \mu_o\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}$" — Gaussian setup for tractable analysis
- L51-52 (SFT objective): "$L_{SFT}(\beta) := KL(p_n \| q_\beta)$" — Forward-KL objective for supervised fine-tuning with new-only data
- L56-57 (RL objective): "$L_{RL}(\beta, m_o, m_n) := KL(q_{\beta,m_o,m_n} \| p_\alpha)$" — Reverse-KL objective toward explicit target retaining old behavior
- L169 (Mahalanobis separation): "$\delta := \|\mu_n - \mu_o\|_{\Sigma^{-1}} = \sqrt{(\mu_n - \mu_o)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_n - \mu_o)}$" — Dimensionless separation metric controlling overlap

## Headline results

- L63-90 (Table 1 summary): "Forward-KL (SFT) exhibits mass forgetting ($\beta^\star = 0$) but controls drift; Reverse-KL (RL) prevents mass forgetting and controls drift exponentially small in $\delta$" — Main theoretical findings across methods
- L239-243 (Theorem 2.1): "$L_{SFT}(\beta) > 0$ for every $\beta \in (0,1]$ and $\beta = 0$ is the unique global minimizer over $[0,1]$" — Forward-KL provably drives old mass to zero
- L257-259 (Forward-KL gradient): "The leakage bound yields $\mathbb{E}_{Y \sim p_n}[r_o(Y)] \leq \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}} \exp(-\delta^2/8)$" — Exponentially small responsibility under mode separation
- L321-322 (Reverse-KL drift bound): "We have $\|\nabla_{m_o} L_{RL}(\beta, \mu_o, m_n)\| \leq \beta \|\Sigma^{-1}\|_2 (\varepsilon_q(\beta,m_n)\|m_n - \mu_o\| + \varepsilon_p(\alpha)\|\mu_n - \mu_o\|)$" — Old mean gradient bounded by overlap quantities

## Limitations

- L41 (Model simplicity): "This setting is intentionally minimal: there are only two modes, the model family is expressive enough to represent both, and yet the aforementioned forms of forgetting can still occur" — Analyzes simplified two-mode case
- L156 (Extensions mentioned): "Many of the forthcoming result assume mixture-of-two Gaussians for simplicity of exposition; extensions to finite-mixture and strongly log-concave densities are provided in Appendices D and E respectively" — Limited to specific distributional assumptions
- L764 (Future work): "Future work includes extending this analysis to high-dimensional generative models where behaviors correspond to richer semantic modes rather than mixture models" — Does not address realistic generative models

## Assumptions and conditions

- L25-29 (Assumption): "We assume that the component $q_o$ has already been trained to approximate the old distribution $p_o$" — Presupposes correct old component representation
- L48-49 (Shared covariance): "We use a shared covariance $\Sigma$ (with bounded spectrum) for both modes so that separation and overlap are controlled purely by the means and the mixture weights" — Equal-covariance Gaussians for tractability
- L373 (Coverage assumption Lemma 2.3): "a minibatch drawn from the current model $q_\beta$ may contain no old-mode samples when $\beta$ is small" — Motivates replay to ensure old-mode visibility
- L540-543 (SDFT separation condition): "if along the EMA+demonstrator trajectory the teacher remains uniformly separated from the old mode $\inf_{t \geq 0} \|\nu_t - \mu_o\|_{\Sigma^{-1}} \geq \underline{\delta} > 0$" — Mode separation condition for drift bounds

## Dimensions

**Evidence**: L233-243 (Theorem 2.1) proves forward-KL drives mass forgetting under population dynamics; L289-305 (Theorem 2.2) proves reverse-KL is consistent at target mixture — "proven" theoretical results with stated Gaussian assumptions

**Measures**: L18-19 asks about quantifying when forgetting occurs; L206-207 defines definitions of mass forgetting and drift; L201 defines $\varepsilon$-bounded drift — "monitorability" of forgetting through gradient signals and convergence behavior

**Elicitation**: L43-46 contrasts forward-KL on new-only data vs reverse-KL toward explicit target; L370 (replay Lemma 2.3) ensures old-mode samples appear in minibatches — "elicited" through post-training objectives and curriculum choices

**Pressure**: L43-46 distinguishes forward-KL (new-only SFT) vs reverse-KL (on-policy RL); L93-96 (replay effect) shows numerator replay modifies training distribution for SFT but reverse-KL remains unchanged — different "training-incidental" and "monitor-in-reward" pressures produce different forgetting signatures
