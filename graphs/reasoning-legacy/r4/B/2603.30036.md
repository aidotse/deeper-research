## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — aligned / orthogonal / in-conflict framework — "We now introduce the main contribution of our paper: a conceptual framework for reasoning about whether an RL environment will cause issues for CoT monitorability." (L86, under heading "4 The Aligned / Orthogonal / In-Conflict Framework", L84)
- `method summary`: corrected — Models post-training reward as R_out (final output) + R_cot (CoT text). The key idea is that R_cot shapes the CoT text while R_out shapes the computation. A reward pair is classified before training as in-conflict (text scoring high on R_cot cannot describe the computation needed for high R_out), orthogonal (some high-R_cot text can describe it) or aligned (high-R_cot text necessarily describes computation that improves R_out). Everything rests on an assumed inductive bias towards transparent CoT. The authors classify 9 reward pairs in 2 toy environments a priori, then train policies with token-level GRPO (CAPO) and in-context RL, select the best policy from each hyperparameter sweep, and measure monitorability. The monitorability loss is induced by the authors' own reward terms. The draft's sentence about snippet search is a process note and should be dropped. — "Our framework allows us to classify these two terms as “aligned”, “orthogonal”, or “in-conflict” before training." (L10); "Importantly, all classifications were made before we ran any of the experiments presented in section 6." (L150)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — Heads-And-Tails (coin state-tracking, N=6 flips; N=42 for the aligned test) and Backdoor-Easy (modified MBPP; output must be correct and backdoored). Policies: Qwen-3-14B trained with CAPO (Gemma-3 in the appendix), and Claude Opus 4.5 optimised by in-context RL. R_cot terms: length penalty, CoT monitor, human-preference grader, punish-string, process supervision. — "We present results for Qwen-3-14B in this section, with Gemma-3 results in appendix E." (L230); "We also make use of an in-context RL algorithm (Moeini et al., 2025) using Claude Opus 4.5 as the policy model." (L232)
- `metric`: corrected — monitorability = score of a prompted LLM CoT monitor that, given only the CoT, must classify whether the reasoning is about a coin (Heads-And-Tails) or involves a backdoor (Backdoor-Easy). Secondary metric: the proportion of runs scoring >0.8 on both R_cot and R_out. — "The task we consider is whether a CoT monitor given only $c$ can correctly classify which type of task the model’s reasoning corresponds to:" (L168); "We compare the proportion of runs that achieve a score of $>0.8$ on both $R_{\text{cot}}$ and $R_{\text{out}}$ across our training runs" (L236)
- `result`: corrected — The a-priori classes predicted the outcomes across 9 reward pairs in 2 environments. In-conflict rewards drove monitorability to near zero, while orthogonal and aligned rewards preserved it. In-conflict rewards were also harder to optimise: fewer seeds reached a high score on both terms, and all Gemma failures were on in-conflict pairs. The aligned reward (process supervision) improved R_out at N=42 flips without hurting monitorability. The prediction that aligned rewards improve monitorability was not tested, because monitorability was already perfect. The length-penalty drop in Backdoor-Easy was trivial: the model output empty reasoning. — "in-conflict rewards lead to near-zero monitorability, while orthogonal and aligned rewards preserve it." (L246); "Note that we could not test the framework’s prediction that these rewards actively improve monitorability, since monitorability is already perfect at the start of training in our setting." ## Limitations and conditions

- "Our definitions rest on the assumption that models have a strong inductive bias towards transparency due to pre-training on human language" (L94) — core assumption behind every classification.
- "It is important to note that if a policy does not achieve high enough $R_{\text{cot}}$ and $R_{\text{out}}$ performance, we will not see issues with transparency." (L136) — in-conflict reduces monitorability only when optimised well.
- "We regard the claims that aligned rewards improve transparency as more speculative, since (1) we do not test the claim empirically" (L132) — the aligned prediction is untested.
- "For rewards classified as in conflict, we separately consider whether they are in conflict with respect to transparency and with respect to our monitoring tasks." (L182) — the class depends on the monitoring task. "This makes being in conflict with respect to a monitoring task a stronger condition than in the transparency case" (L144).
- "We believe that in Heads-And-Tails, token-based length penalties are likely still orthogonal for monitorability." (L208) — the length-penalty class depends on how length is measured.
- "we run hyperparameter sweeps and select the policy that performed highest on $R_{\text{cot}}+R_{\text{out}}$ across our sweep." (L222) — monitorability is reported for the best-optimised policy per pair, not for an average run.
- "Length penalty caused only trivial unmonitorability in Backdoor-Easy." (L250) — the drop came from empty reasoning, not obfuscation.
- "models slowly drop in monitorability when training against some orthogonal rewards with certain prompts" (L252) — semantic drift in ICRL, which the framework does not capture.
- "It is generally hard to prove a negative (i.e. “X cannot be learned”), but we provide several lines of circumstantial evidence for this claim." (L258) — the hard-to-optimise finding is circumstantial.
- "we remained in toy environments largely because of the difficulty of learning against in-conflict rewards in more complex settings." (L262) — scope; CAPO was adopted after standard GRPO failed.
- "Our environments are relatively simple, and hence perhaps our empirical results do not generalize to real LLM post-training." (L266) — external validity.
- "For example, many modern models are trained with length penalties without any observed drop in monitorability (Guan et al., 2025)." (L268) — whether a drop appears in practice is unclear.
- "we expect in many cases it may be unclear which category a pair of rewards falls into" (L270) — classifying reward pairs is a judgement, not a measurement.
- "Chain-of-Thought semantics might change during long training runs even without concrete incentives." (L272) — transparency drift.
- "an encouraging sign for the viability of CoT monitoring, though not a guarantee against more capable optimization in the future." (L276) — limit on the optimisation-difficulty finding.

## Other findings

- The paper is by Google DeepMind authors. The monitorability task is narrow: a binary topic classification (coin or backdoor) by a prompted monitor whose prompts were "manually optimized on a set of example reasoning traces" (L178).
- The draft says orthogonal and aligned terms are "predicted" not to hurt or to improve monitorability. The orthogonal prediction and the "aligned does not hurt" part were tested. The "aligned improves" part was not.
- The authors draw a practical implication that the draft omits: "length penalties and preference-based rewards, commonly used in post-training, can be in conflict under our framework and may undermine monitorability" (L276).
