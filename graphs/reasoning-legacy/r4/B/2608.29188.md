## Limitations and conditions

- "Exhaustive enumeration is what makes access and execution separately measurable, which currently ties the direct analysis to Countdown." (L249) — the access/execution decomposition is direct only on Countdown.
- "Our PPO run uses rollout group size $G{=}1$ , no actor KL penalty, and a 1,024-token training rollout cap" (L249) — the self-trained PPO run has no KL penalty, which may exaggerate contraction relative to standard setups.
- "the second run is a public GRPO series we evaluate rather than train; and SFT controls use LoRA fine-tuning" (L249) — the GRPO side is not under the authors' control.
- "On standard math benchmarks the first complete calculation is an observable proxy for an entrance, not a solver-defined one." (L249) — the 7B/14B generalisation rests on a proxy.
- "The supplied-entrance quantity $E_{t}^{\mathrm{do}}$ is interventional: it measures completion after an entrance is set externally" (L249) — "remains executable" is measured under intervention, not the policy's own execution term.
- "Because the un-tuned base model generates $<3\%$ format-valid outputs, step 50 serves as the earliest checkpoint" (L87) — the 67% drop is measured from step 50, not from the base model.
- "In Countdown, selecting an entrance leaves only 6–10 tokens of downstream computation, making entrance access near-exclusive determinant of success." (L207) — the "lost at the entrance" result is partly a property of Countdown's short horizon; on long problems "long-horizon execution acts as a compounding secondary bottleneck" (L207).
- "although with single-sample accuracy shifting toward the early checkpoint" (L193) — checkpoint sampling's larger coverage gain (+0.100) costs pass@1 (0.161 vs 0.278, L187).
- "At $\text{pass@1 }=0.333$ , SFT maintains a solution coverage of $0.709$" (L241) — the SFT comparison is at lower accuracy and uses solver-enumerated multi-solution demonstrations, which are only available for enumerable tasks.
- "Higher temperature recovers some breadth, and no setting reaches the step-50 coverage of 0.337." (L391) — decoding cannot undo the contraction.
- "Because rarely sampled entrances receive diminishing gradient signals, initial sampling asymmetries compound over training" (L79) — the proposed mechanism rests on a first-order surrogate (Appendix B.2), not on direct measurement.

## Other findings

- For cluster C, the paper sides with "select/narrow": at 7B and 14B, SimpleRL leaves pass@64 essentially unchanged while pass@1 rises (L203), and late-policy alternatives remain executable when prompted (L163, L207). It also argues narrowing is not inevitable: staged SFT-DPO-RLVR (OLMo-3) and distillation keep first-calculation entropy (L209).
- The layer-interpolation pass@1 change is +0.026 with a CI crossing zero ([-0.009, 0.065], L185), so "no loss in pass@1" means no significant loss, not a gain.
- The conclusion says entrance-targeted interventions "restore up to 38% of solution coverage" (L245), while the body gives 37% for layer interpolation (L193).
- The draft omits the entrance-budgeted test-time allocation (+0.077 coverage over free resampling, L195), an intervention that needs no early checkpoint but does need a solver to identify feasible entrances.
