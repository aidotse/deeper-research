## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — Depth-Recurrent Transformer ("vertical Chain-of-Thought"), trained with silent thinking — "In this work, we study an orthogonal paradigm that we call vertical Chain-of-Thought: instead of generating tokens horizontally, the model “thinks deeper” by recurrently applying a shared-weight Transformer block." - `method summary`: corrected — A task-specific perception interface encodes the input, a single shared-weight Pre-LN Transformer block (under 1M parameters) is applied T times with a depth embedding and a GRU-like gate whose bias is initialised to -2.0, and a task-specific readout decodes the final state. Loss is computed only at the final step, with T sampled uniformly from a task-specific range during training; at inference T is an externally set hyperparameter (no learned halting). LayerScale (init 1e-4) is used for the boolean and text tasks but omitted for the graph task. Memory is flat and latency linear in T, compared against the KV cache of thinking tokens on a GPT-2-medium-sized backbone. — "we compute the cross-entropy (CE) loss $\text{CE}(\hat{y},\,y)$ only on the final thinking step $T$" (L144); "For the topological interface, where the hard adjacency mask strongly constrains the computation, we omit LayerScale" (L140); "At inference time, we treat the recurrence step count $T$ as an external hyperparameter" (L401)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — three synthetic tasks, each with a hand-chosen perception interface: graph reachability (adjacency-masked attention; train 1-5 hops, test to 12), nested boolean expressions (RoPE; train depth 1-8), and a CLUTRR-inspired family relational-composition task over shuffled text with distractors and parity-matched hard negatives (train depth 2-5, test to 9); tiny models (0.2-1.0M params) trained from scratch; baselines a fixed-depth GNN, fixed-depth Transformers (0.8-6.4M), and an ACT Universal Transformer — "we evaluate it on a family-vocabulary relational composition task inspired by CLUTRR (Sinha et al., 2019)" (L287); "We deliberately study reasoning cores of fewer than 1M parameters" (L393)

## Limitations and conditions

- "Whether identity-biased gating and LayerScale still suffice as $d$ , $d_{\mathrm{ff}}$ , and $L$ grow by orders of magnitude remains open." (L393) — all results are on cores under 1M parameters, trained from scratch on synthetic tasks.
- "Our three interfaces (topological masking, RoPE, standard attention) were hand-selected per task" (L397) — the generalization profiles depend on a hand-chosen task-specific inductive bias.
- "At inference time, we treat the recurrence step count $T$ as an external hyperparameter" (L401) — results assume an oracle-set step budget; learned halting (ACT) failed to extrapolate.
- "the learned halting budget is calibrated to the training distribution and does not extrapolate" (L401) — adaptive-depth variant collapses to chance OOD on graph.
- "This boundary is capacity-dependent: a wider model propagates several hops further" (L267) — the 8-hop limit is width-dependent.
- "We deliberately widen the dimension to $d=256$ because with smaller capacity ( $d=128$ ), neither objective propagates past $8$ hops." (L333) — the harm of per-step supervision appears only at larger width, and only on the graph task.
- "though accuracy falls toward chance by depth 9" (L293) — the unstructured text task generalizes only partially OOD.
- "ablating our stability recipe (last row: no LayerScale, no gate) leaves ID and mean OOD accuracy essentially unchanged" (L323) — the three stability ingredients buy seed stability, not accuracy.
- "We compare our silent-thinking objective against an intermediate-supervision baseline on the graph reachability task" (L327) — the supervision finding is tested on one task.
