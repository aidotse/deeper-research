## Limitations and conditions

- "we underscore that these results only represent two experiments and more thorough study is needed." (L327) — the RL-does-not-degrade result rests on two runs.
- "observe a considerable decrease in the middle of RL training for GPT-5.1 Codex Max" (L327) — the RL result is not monotone; it tracks thinking-token count.
- "All of these trends in monitorability largely correlate with the amount of thinking tokens the model uses over the course of training." (L327) — the RL trend may be a CoT-length effect.
- "suggesting our metric does not properly account for problems that are noise-dominated and that we likely need to revise our thresholding." (L327) — known metric weakness, seen in the sandbagging eval.
- "While our results suggest that reinforcement learning does not meaningfully degrade CoT monitorability at the current frontier scale, it still very well could at increased scale." (L385) — scope of the RL result.
- "One limitation of these results is that we only tested monitorability with OpenAI models serving as monitors." (L301) — the OpenAI-vs-external comparison may be inflated by shared idiosyncrasies.
- "For this plot, we only ran the subset of our evals that do not require tools." (L16) — the cross-vendor comparison is restricted to non-tool environments.
- "This excludes models such as the Gemini-2.5/3 series and Claude 4.x series which only share summarized reasoning in their API." (L293) — external model coverage.
- "Our experiments are relatively limited as we did not do broad sweeps or ablations, so we do not make any strong claims." (L311) — the obfuscation-detection result.
- "Most of this decrease seems to be primarily driven by drastically reduced CoT lengths" (L309) — the generic obfuscation model was caught mainly through shorter CoTs.
- "A major limitation of these experiments is that we only trained with a single seed at each model scale." (L341) — pretraining scaling; the "medium" model is off-trend.
- "our results only consider capability evaluations that are somewhat in-distribution for these models" (L341) — the monitorability-tax trade-off may not hold out of distribution.
- "we did not consider potential latency costs associated with exchanging shorter and less monitorable CoTs" (L341) — monitorability tax ignores latency.
- "However, in all of these cases, it requires more inference compute to do so" (L337) — condition on the smaller-model monitorability gain.
- "Our evaluations do not disentangle the propensity to verbalize versus the necessity to verbalize" (L381) — limits interpretation of low sycophancy monitorability.
- "However, there are many other processes that we did not investigate that we leave to future work." (L383) — e.g. monitor-guided environment fixing and deliberative alignment as slow obfuscation pressure.
- "We did not calibrate in this way and leave it to future work" (L285) — outcome-property results are not FPR-calibrated; low g-mean² there is driven by low TNR.
- "These instances are misleadingly labeled as false positives, so we filter a subset of the distribution as a short term solution" (L283) — label noise and filtering in ShadeArena and OAI-repo-sabotage.
- "All of our evaluations require that the model first do a specific behavior before we can ask if a monitor can detect that behavior." (L74) — evals with too little behaviour are dropped, so aggregates across figures may not be comparable.
- "we drop an intervention dataset if the number of problem instances with $\mathrm{TE}\leq 0$ exceeds 90% of the dataset" (L187) — filtering condition on the intervention metric.
- "Under monotonicity (i.e., $\Pr(Y=1|X=1)\geq\Pr(Y=1|X=0)$ ), the total effect lower-bounds the mass of such cases" (L138) — assumption behind the minimal-criterion TPR.
- "it cannot guarantee that no additional solution paths exist beyond current knowledge" (L249) — process-eval ground truth assumption.
- "Thus, for our $\text{g-mean}^{2}$ calculation for this archetype, we only consider the sensitivity (TPR), setting specificity (TNR) to 1." (L251) — process evals have no sound negatives.
- "In this setting, follow-up questions can actively degrade monitorability." (L359) — follow-up questions fail on subtle-framing interventions such as Scruples.

## Other findings

- The paper is from OpenAI and most results use OpenAI models as both agent and monitor; the external-model comparison is limited to models exposing full reasoning and to non-tool environments.
- The draft's RL claim reads as unconditional ("monitorability survives this optimisation pressure"). The paper frames it as two runs, a mid-run dip for GPT-5.1 Codex Max, and a trend tied to CoT length.
- The draft omits the follow-up-question result (Sec. 7) and the size of the monitor-scaling effects (~40% across monitors; ~12% vs ~4% for o4-mini effort scaling).
- The draft's `method summary` contains a process note about snippet search that is not about the paper.
