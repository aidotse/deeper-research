## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — Monotone Frontier Curriculum (MFC). It is the paper's proposed method within a teacher-guided backward-chaining framework. The headline data-efficiency result is shared with two other curricula, AdaBack and Mixture (R3). — "propose Monotone Frontier Curriculum (MFC), a method that monotonically drives training toward unguided solving" (line 11)
- `method summary`: corrected — Selects problems with pass@64 = 0 for the student and has a teacher (DeepSeek-V3.2) write a step-structured trace for each. Problems the teacher also fails are discarded. A guidance level ρ reveals the first round(ρ·n_steps) steps as a hint in the prompt; the student then writes a full solution under GRPO. MFC keeps one guidance frontier per problem and lowers it only after a success (mean reward ≥ τ = 0.5). Failures leave the frontier unchanged, so the frontier never rises. This raises the share of training on unguided prompts, which the paper argues matters because guided prompts are a distribution shift from test time. AdaBack, by contrast, also raises a lower bound on failure. — "MFC keeps only the success-side ratchet, committing $g_{\mathrm{curr}}\leftarrow g_{\mathrm{used}}$ on a successful visit if smaller, while failures leave the per-sample state untouched and still contribute to the GRPO advantage." (line 135); "Whenever $g(s,\rho)>0$ , the curriculum induces a distribution shift between training and test prompts." (line 121)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — Students: Qwen3-1.7B (primary) and Qwen3-0.6B, base checkpoints. Teacher: DeepSeek-V3.2. Training data: OR1-2k, a 2,000-problem subset of OpenR1-Math-220k, for GRPO; and Uns-128, the 128 shortest of the 136 teacher-solvable problems among the 147 that are 64-unsolvable for Qwen3-1.7B, for the curricula. Uns-128 is reused unchanged for 0.6B. Nine evaluation benchmarks: OR1-200 and Uns-22 (in-domain); MATH-500, AMC23 and AIME24/25/26 (out-of-distribution math); SciBench and GPQA-Diamond (non-math). — "We use Qwen3-1.7B Yang et al. ## Limitations and conditions

- "Our work relies on a single fixed teacher model (DeepSeek-V3.2) to generate all reasoning traces, and the quality of the resulting curriculum is inevitably tied to the quality of these traces." (line 289) — every result depends on one teacher.
- "We do not study how teacher capability interacts with curriculum design." (line 289) — untested variable.
- "The framework is trained only on mathematical reasoning with single-turn LLM outputs." (line 291) — scope.
- "SciBench and GPQA-Diamond provide out-of-domain probes, but they do not constitute a study of scientific reasoning." (line 291) — the non-math transfer claim is weak by the authors' own account.
- "scientific reasoning often lacks the discrete step structure our hint mechanism relies on" (line 291) — assumption: teacher traces split into discrete steps.
- "Transfer to code generation Li et al. (2022) or formal theorem proving is untested." (line 291) — scope.
- "discard problems on which the teacher itself fails (136 remaining), and select the $128$ shortest by combined question-plus-trace length" (line 199) — the "unsolvable" set is filtered to problems the teacher can solve, and biased toward short ones.
- "The same Uns-128 is reused unchanged when training Qwen3-0.6B (a fortiori unsolvable for the weaker sibling)." (line 199) — unsolvability was measured on 1.7B only; for 0.6B it is assumed.
- "all run for $1{,}200$ GRPO steps under matched compute" (line 203) — "~16× data efficiency" counts unique source problems. Training compute is matched; the teacher's trace generation is not counted.
- "The $2.2\%$ residual means that the per-problem success probability is small but not strictly zero" (line 87) — "unsolvable" is a finite-sample filter (pass@64 = 0), not true zero probability.
- "Mixture is competitive on Qwen3-1.7B but trails GRPO on the Qwen3-0.6B average ( $30.6$ vs $31.3$ ), so uniform- $\rho$ mixing alone is not enough at the smaller scale." (line 217) — a condition on the headline "for both base models".
- "Empirically, this increase is associated with a higher cross-benchmark average and broader reasoning coverage" (line 247) — the link from unguided training mass to the gains is stated as an association.

## Other findings

- For cluster C (create vs select), the relevant point is that the reasoning-boundary gain comes from a stronger model's solution traces placed in the prompt. It is not RL alone. The paper argues it is "not attributable to teacher-trace distillation" (line 265), on the strength of one SFT baseline. That baseline is weak: Qwen3-1.7B only, a single full trace per problem, 50 epochs on 128 problems. So it rules out naive SFT on these traces, not distillation in general. A reader should not cite this paper as RL creating capability without a teacher.
- The pass@k evidence rests on 90 AIME problems. In the mapped places, no seeds, confidence intervals or multiple training runs are reported.
- On the 0.6B model, MFC's averages are below GRPO's on OR1-200 (50.4 vs 51.1) and MATH-500 (76.8 vs 78.8) (lines 181, 187). "Matches or exceeds" is a statement about the average.
- The non-math transfer is "+1.2 to +4.4 pp over Base" (line 227), measured against the untrained Base, not against GRPO. On Qwen3-1.7B, GRPO is below Base on GPQA-Diamond (33.8 vs 34.9), and MFC is at 36.1 (lines 169-177).
