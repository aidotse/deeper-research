## Method
- L39-42: "The coverage profile of a model $\widehat{\pi}$ for a distribution $\pi$ is $\texttt{Cov}_{N}(\pi\;\|\;\widehat{\pi})\vcentcolon=\mathbb{P}_{x\sim\mathcal{\mu},y\sim\pi(\cdot\mid{}x)}\left[\frac{\pi(y\mid{}x)}{\widehat{\pi}(y\mid{}x)}\geq{}N\right]$" — defines coverage profile, a key metric for characterizing when next-token prediction enables post-training.
- L50: "Next-token prediction implicitly optimizes toward a model with good coverage" — the coverage principle showing that maximum likelihood learning inherits coverage from training data.
- L56-62: "Next-token prediction (more generally, maximum likelihood) that exploits the unique structure of the logarithmic loss to show that coverage can generalize faster than cross-entropy; we refer to this as the coverage principle" — proposes that coverage generalizes faster than cross-entropy.
- L64-72: "Explore families of new interventions aimed at improving coverage in theory. (i) Test-time (Section 6.1). (ii) Model/checkpoint selection (Section 6.3). For selecting the best model (or checkpoint) from a small number of candidates, we give tournament procedures that enjoy significantly better coverage profile" — discusses test-time and checkpoint selection methods for improving coverage.

## Headline results
- L44-46: "The coverage profile is correlated with downstream performance for Best-of-N (which is exactly $\mathsf{Pass@}{N}$ ), even when cross-entropy is not" — coverage profile predicts Best-of-N performance better than KL divergence.
- L208-210: "Next-token prediction achieves $\displaystyle\texttt{Cov}_{N}(\widehat{\pi})\lesssim\frac{d\left[\left[\log(BH/\log N)\right]_{+}+\frac{\log(BHn)}{\log N}\right]+\log(1/\delta)}{n}$" — coverage bound for autoregressive linear models showing near-horizon-independent convergence.
- L222: "$\displaystyle\En\left[\texttt{Cov}_{N}(\widehat{\pi})\right]\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\star}^{2}}{n\cdot\log N}}+\frac{B^{2}}{n}$" — overparameterized model coverage bound with inherent variance.
- L317-319: "The normalized SGD update (18) achieves the following coverage bound: $\displaystyle\En\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\texttt{Cov}_{N}(\pi_{\theta^{{\scriptscriptstyle t}}})\right]\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\star}^{2}}{T\cdot\log N}}+\frac{B^{2}}{T}$" — gradient normalization achieves horizon-independent coverage.

## Limitations
- L437-442: "Our formulation in Section 2 is a simplification (one that is closer in spirit to supervised fine-tuning), but we expect that the insights derived here can extend to the general setting" — simplifying assumption that pre-training has explicit prompts and responses.
- L442-443: "Much of our analysis focuses on the realizable/well-specified setting where $\pi_{\texttt{D}}\in\Pi$. We give evidence in Appendix E that the coverage profile is more tolerant to misspecification than KL-divergence, but we leave a deeper investigation for future work" — mostly analyzes realizable case.
- L445-447: "Our treatment assumes the distribution over prompts $\mathcal{\mu}$ is the same for pre-training and post-training. This is straightforward to relax at the cost of introducing an additional coverage or distribution shift coefficient" — assumes prompt distribution matches between pre- and post-training.
- L450-451: "While there is ample evidence current RL techniques can fail in the absence of coverage (Yue et al., 2025; Gandhi et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025), it is not clear what the minimal conditions required for RL are" — unclear what conditions are minimal for RL success.
- L453-454: "Our results focus on coverage at the sequence level. For reasoning tasks, it is natural to explicitly factorize the response $y=(y_{\texttt{cot}},y_{\texttt{ans}})$ into a chain-of-thought (reasoning trajectory) component $y_{\texttt{cot}}$ and an answer component $y_{\texttt{ans}}$" — sequence-level focus doesn't account for intermediate reasoning.
