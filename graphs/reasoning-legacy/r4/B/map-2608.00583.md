# Map of 2608.00583: A False Average: Pooled CoT-Monitor Accuracy Conceals a Reasoning-Dependent Fragility

## Method
- L16-21 (CoT Monitoring Fragility): "Chain-of-thought (CoT) monitors are reported by their aggregate accuracy on a pool of reward hacks. We show that this number is a false average." — method exposes aggregate metrics hiding subset-level fragility
- L22-23 (Rewrite Attack): "To measure the subset on its own we need an adversary who controls only the reasoning. A monitor that leans on the reasoning can be only as trustworthy as the reasoning is hard to control." — adversary model of gradient-free reasoning rewrite
- L23 (One-Shot Gradient-Free Rewrite): "a single gradient-free rewrite collapses a held-out monitor's catch rate on the subset from about 95% to between 4 and 11%" — method uses rewriting without training
- L72-73 (Threat Model): "Our threat model gives the attacker control of nothing but the narration. It rewrites only agent reasoning text; every tool call and observation is copied byte-for-byte" — precise control boundary for isolation
- L78 (Attack Procedure): "We give an LLM, the obfuscator (gpt-5.4), a caught hack and ask it to rewrite the agent's reasoning so a CoT monitor would read it as good-faith engineering, changing no command or output." — uses LLM for obfuscation

## Setup
- L20 (Dataset): "On Terminal Wrench (Bercovich et al., 2026) about 77% of hacks give themselves away in the actions however narrated" — uses Terminal Wrench benchmark
- L66 (Task Description): "Terminal Wrench is a dataset of agent trajectories on command-line coding tasks, each labeled as an honest solve or a reward hack" — coding task dataset with hack labels
- L68 (Monitor Setup): "We drive the dataset's own LLM judge unchanged; it scores a trajectory from 0 (an honest solve) to 1 (a clear exploit)" — uses dataset's original monitor
- L68-69 (Held-Out Monitor): "The rewrite targets one monitor (M0, gpt-5.4-nano) and is scored by a held-out monitor from a different model family (M1, claude-sonnet-4.6)" — evaluates transfer across monitor families
- L70-71 (Reasoning-Dependent Subset): "A hack is reasoning-dependent if the target monitor M0 (nano, full scope) catches it at its 5% FPR threshold and the held-out M1 (sonnet, action-only scope) misses it at its own" — defines subset where reasoning is only signal

## Headline results
- L78 (Attack Success): "On the reasoning-dependent subset the held-out monitor's catch rate falls from 96.2% to 3.8% (8 of 209; 95% CI 2 to 7%)" — dramatic collapse in subset-specific performance
- L79 (Aggregate Effect): "On an unbiased random 150 of the caught pool the same rewrite drops the target monitor M0 from 1.00 to 0.78 and the held-out M1 from 0.98 to 0.73" — much smaller aggregate effect due to pooling
- L86 (Robustness Under Skeptical Rubric): "Held-out monitor, skeptic rubric — 96% original, 4% obfuscated" — attack holds under tougher instructions
- L90 (Paraphrase Control): "Paraphrase control (no evade intent) — 91% original, 85% obfuscated" — meaning-preserving paraphrase barely degrades
- L92 (Different Actor Family): "Different actor family (opus hacks) — 74% original, 0% obfuscated" — transfers across model families
- L107 (External Ground Truth Defense): "a genuine reference solution to the same task lifts catch to 48% (72 of 150)" — only external information partially recovers detection
- L132-133 (Live Agent Results): "Under the narration instruction the three agents produce 38, 68 and 21 hollow hacks" and "The monitor ranks them above the model's own honest solves at AUC 0.996, 1.000 and 1.000, and at the calibrated threshold it misses none" — live agents produce innocent narration unaided but monitor still catches them

## Limitations
- L156 (Task Scope): "The live-agent study bounds the spontaneous threat on 27 tasks from one task family and one benchmark" — limited to one benchmark family
- L156 (Sample Size): "hollow hacks arise in only 11 to 19 of them per agent" — low counts in some conditions
- L157 (Residual Counts): "The low-scoring residual rests on counts of 7, 7 and 2, too few to fix as a rate" — too few examples to establish robust rates
- L157 (Calibration Concern): "the calibrated threshold lands on a tie and the realized false-positive rate is 8 to 26%, not 5%" — threshold sensitivity to rare outcomes
- L157 (Benchmark Specificity): "We study a single benchmark and one monitor-rubric family with LLM-judge monitors" — limited generalization
- L157-158 (Rewrite Not Applied to Live): "The frozen-pool attack rests on four independent obfuscator draws rather than repeated judge seeds, which temperature 0 makes uninformative" — cannot apply rewrite to live agent's own hacks

## Assumptions and conditions
- L21 (Hack Assumption): "On Terminal Wrench about 77% of hacks give themselves away in the actions however narrated" — assumes most hacks are action-visible
- L22 (Action-Only Baseline): "unattacked, the monitor catches 96% of the subset" — assumes high baseline catch rate on reasoning-dependent subset
- L24 (Action Faithfulness Assumption): "An independent judge of every claim about commands and outputs rates the rewrite as action-faithful as the honest original, 0.96 versus 0.97" — rewrite maintains factual fidelity to actions
- L25 (Dilution Prediction): "Dilution predicts a 21-point pooled drop (23% of hacks losing 92 points); we observe 25." — validates that dilution model explains aggregate effect
- L101 (Faithfulness of Rewrite): "the rewrite lies exactly there" (about intent) but "is truthful about what happened" — assumes rewrite is internally consistent

## Dimensions
- **evidence** — L11-13 (empirical): "The CoT is most useful in cases where a check on the agent's actions alone is not sufficient to distinguish between satisfying a specification and gaming it" and "We show that this number is a false average" — empirical finding about monitor performance
- **measures** — L11 (faithfulness and monitorability): "it rests crucially on the faithfulness of the CoT" — measures both faithfulness (internal consistency with actions) and monitorability (ability to detect hacks)
- **elicitation** — L72-73 (elicited, gradient-free): "Our threat model gives the attacker control of nothing but the narration" — elicited obfuscation through LLM rewriting without training
- **pressure** — L72-73 (prompt-level): "It rewrites only agent reasoning text; every tool call and observation is copied byte-for-byte" — prompt-level attack with no training, weakest threat model
