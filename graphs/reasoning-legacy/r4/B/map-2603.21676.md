## Method

- L3-8 (Title and Abstract): "Thinking Deeper, Not Longer: Memory-Efficient Test-Time Reasoning with Depth-Recurrent Transformers for Compositional Generalization. We study a depth-recurrent Transformer that decouples computational depth from parameter count by iterating a shared-weight block, so that each added reasoning step costs flat memory and linear latency" — introduces depth-recurrent approach as alternative to horizontal CoT.
- L101-105 (Section 3): "Our architecture consists of three components: a task-invariant reasoning core that iteratively refines a hidden representation through shared-weight recurrence, a task-specific perception interface that encodes the raw input into this representation's initial state, and a task-specific readout head" — the three-component architecture.
- L108-138 (Section 3.1): "The reasoning core is a single Transformer block with shared weights, applied recurrently for T steps... To ensure that this recurrent core can stably unroll for 20+ steps and learn genuine algorithms, we detail its architecture below and design it around three additional stability mechanisms" — describes the reasoning core with Pre-LayerNorm, LayerScale, and identity-biased gating.
- L141-145 (Section 3.1): "Silent Thinking Objective: A fundamental design choice is how to supervise the recurrent steps. Instead of forcing intermediate answers or applying per-step auxiliary losses, we compute the cross-entropy loss only on the final thinking step T" — training objective that applies supervision only at final step.

## Setup

- L37-39 (Section 1): "We design three OOD reasoning tasks. We begin with Graph Reachability, which provides a physically verifiable testbed using topological masking. We then increase the structural complexity with Nested Boolean Logic, demonstrating the model's ability to maintain hierarchical states using relative positioning. Finally, we remove all task-aligned structural inductive biases in a Relational Composition task over unstructured text" — the three evaluation tasks.
- L261-263 (Section 4.1): "Given a directed graph G=(V,E), this task determines whether there exists a directed path from node s to r. The model is trained on instances requiring 1–5 hops with 5–8 thinking steps and is evaluated up to 12 hops" — graph reachability task specifics.
- L273-275 (Section 4.2): "Given a fragile nested boolean expression (e.g., !((T&F)|(!(T|F)))), this task evaluates it to True or False. Training succeeds on nesting depths 1–8" — nested boolean logic task.
- L286-290 (Section 4.3): "we evaluate it on a family-vocabulary relational composition task inspired by CLUTRR, where the correct relation is defined by a signed-offset algebra over parent/child hops... The model is trained on depths 2–5 with 1–12 thinking steps, and evaluated up to depth 9 and 20 thinking steps" — relational composition task.
- L238-239 (Section 4): "Table 2 summarizes the architectural hyperparameters used for each experiment" — includes hidden dimension 128/256, feedforward dimension 256/1024, and training step ranges.

## Headline results

- L39-40 (Section 1): "We find a computational frontier: accuracy climbs once the thinking-step count meets the task's complexity, reaching near-perfect performance on the two structured tasks and a lower plateau on unstructured text" — main finding about computational frontier.
- L264-267 (Section 4.1): "As illustrated in Figure 2, we observe a sharp computational frontier. The accuracy transition from chance to perfect/near-perfect: once given enough (N+) thinking steps, the model solves N-hop queries... The model achieves 100% OOD generalization up to 8 hops (1.6×), but collapses abruptly at 10 hops" — graph reachability results.
- L276-278 (Section 4.2): "As depicted in Figure 3, we observe a gradual computational frontier. The model degrades gracefully beyond the training distribution, holding 90%+ accuracy at depth 14 (1.75× OOD)" — nested boolean logic results.
- L294-295 (Section 4.3): "the accuracy decreases overall as the chain depth increases... the invariant core stays above chance at depths 6 and 7 (66%+ once given sufficient steps), and unrolling past the trained range (16–20 steps) neither helps nor hurts" — relational composition results.
- L318 (Section 4.4, Table 3): "Depth-Recurrent (ours): Graph 0.2P, 100% ID, 75±0.2% OOD; Nested Expr. 1.0P, 100% ID, 95±1% OOD; Family 1.0P, 83% ID, 60±1% OOD" — comparison with baselines.

## Limitations

- L332-333 (Section 4.5): "The Attacker model being evaluated is set to Claude Sonnet 3.5, and we run the experiments in the attack and control conditions against both Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Claude 3 Opus Defenders. This requires a total of 512=128×2×2 minigame simulations" — limited evaluation scale.
- L390-392 (Section 5.5): "We deliberately study reasoning cores of fewer than 1M parameters, isolating the recurrence mechanisms from large-scale pretraining confounds... Whether identity-biased gating and LayerScale still suffice as d, d_ff, and L grow by orders of magnitude remains open" — limited to small-scale models.
- L394-396 (Section 5.5): "Our three interfaces (topological masking, RoPE, standard attention) were hand-selected per task; a more general system should unify these choices into one interface" — task-specific perception interfaces require manual design.
- L398-401 (Section 5.5): "At inference time, we treat the recurrence step count T as an external hyperparameter... A practical system would benefit from a lightweight halting module" — requires external specification of step count rather than learned halting.
- L400-401 (Section 5.5): "we found ACT Universal Transformer learns a halting budget that often saturates below the depth OOD instances require, collapsing to chance on graph reachability beyond the training range" — ACT halting doesn't generalize.

## Assumptions and conditions

- L145-146 (Section 3.1): "During training, T is sampled uniformly from a task-specific range (e.g., T∼U(1,12)). This randomized depth acts as a regularizer, encouraging the network to produce stable outputs across a range of computation depths" — randomized training depth as regularizer.
- L176-187 (Section 3.1): "we use a gated recurrence with the gate bias initialized to −2.0. Because σ(−2.0)≈0.12, the model defaults to retaining 88% of its previous state" — gate bias initialization to enable gradient flow.
- L262-263 (Section 4.1): "dashed lines mark the training boundaries in both dimensions (1–5 hops; 5–8 thinking steps); cells outside these ranges are also OOD" — clear in-distribution and out-of-distribution designations.
- L283 (Section 4.2): "Unlike the hard adjacency mask used for graph reachability, RoPE only biases attention toward nearby positions rather than blocking distant ones" — task structure affects information propagation.
- L327-331 (Section 4.5): "We interpolate between two objectives using parameter α. L(α)=(1−α)CE(ŷ(T),y)+(α/T)∑CE(ŷ(t),y), where ŷ(t) is the readout applied to H(t), so α=0 recovers silent thinking; α=1 is per-step supervision" — comparison of supervision strategies.

## Dimensions

- L84-85 (Section 2.5): "By making depth as a dynamic variable through recurrence, our architecture bypasses the TC^0 limitation: the unrolled reasoning steps can scale with the input's intrinsic sequential complexity" — evidence relates to theoretical capability regarding circuit complexity.
- L62 (Section 1): "We identify and analyze the computational frontier phenomenon and show how task-specific perception interfaces yield qualitatively different generalization behaviors" — empirical measures of extrapolation capabilities across different structural biases.
- L27-36 (Section 1): "Three ingredients keep the recurrence stable for 20+ thinking steps: a silent thinking objective that supervises only the final output, LayerScale initialization, and an identity-biased gate that opens a gradient highway across steps" — techniques for training stability during deep unrolling.
- L57-62 (Section 1): "We show that intermediate per-step supervision, a standard recipe for training deep iterative models, harms OOD generalization on the graph task, whose adjacency mask lets us verify how far information has actually propagated. We analytically and empirically show that the recurrent core holds peak memory flat and latency linear in the reasoning-step count" — trade-offs in training approaches and computational efficiency measures.
