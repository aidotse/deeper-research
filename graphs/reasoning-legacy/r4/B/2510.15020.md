- `method summary`: corrected — Learning-theory analysis of maximum likelihood / next-token prediction in a prompt-response setting. It defines the coverage profile, the probability that the data distribution assigns a response at least N times more mass than the model does, and proves it governs Best-of-N success. It then proves generalization bounds showing coverage converges faster than cross-entropy for the empirical maximizer (general classes; autoregressive linear models in low and high dimension), and analyses one-pass SGD and normalized SGD on overparameterized autoregressive linear models. Results rest on realizability (the data distribution lies in the model class), bounded parameter and feature norms, and the premise that the data distribution itself covers the downstream task. — "The data distribution $\pi_{\texttt{D}}$ is realizable by some model $\pi\in\Pi$" (L86); "We therefore posit that the data distribution covers such a downstream task, in the sense that it includes high-reward responses with some bounded-below probability." ## Limitations and conditions

- "{assumption}[Realizability] The data distribution $\pi_{\texttt{D}}$ is realizable by some model $\pi\in\Pi$" (L86) — assumed throughout.
- "Much of our analysis focuses on the realizable/well-specified setting where $\pi_{\texttt{D}}\in\Pi$" (L442) — misspecification is only partly addressed (Appendix E) and left for future work.
- "We therefore posit that the data distribution covers such a downstream task" (L92) — the whole chain to downstream success presupposes the pre-training data already covers the task; coverage is inherited, not created.
- "Our formulation in Section 2 is a simplification (one that is closer in spirit to supervised fine-tuning)" (L438) — real pre-training does not split prompts and responses, and lengths vary.
- "Our treatment assumes the distribution over prompts $\mathcal{\mu}$ is the same for pre-training and post-training." (L446) — no distribution shift between stages.
- "it is not clear what the minimal conditions required for RL are" (L450) — necessity is proven only for Best-of-N, not RL.
- "Our results focus on coverage at the sequence level." (L454) — answer-level coverage (ignoring the CoT) can be strictly smaller and is not the object analysed.
- "We assume $\Theta\subseteq\left\{\theta:\|\theta\|\leq 1\right\}$ is convex, and $\sup_{h,x,y_{1:h}}\|\phi(x,y_{1:h})\|\leq B$" (L103) — the SGD and linear-model results are for autoregressive linear models with a frozen feature map, not transformers.
- "The only drawback (which is fundamental) is that since the class has low capacity, the coarse-grained term dominates for most parameter regimes" (L210) — in low dimension the improvement with N is modest.
- "while $\sigma_{\star}^{2}$ can be as large as $B^{2}H$ in the worst case, we expect it to be smaller in general" (L220) — the high-dimensional "horizon-independence" relies on the inherent variance being small, which is expected rather than shown.
- "understanding the implications of a limited context window is beyond the scope of this work" (L304) — the SGD model takes a full sequence per step.
- "though the dependence on $\varepsilon$ for Section 5.2 is worse" (L320) — gradient normalization's rate is worse in epsilon than the MLE guarantee, and needs minibatching.

## Other findings

- The draft's claim that coverage is "necessary and sufficient for post-training and test-time methods" overstates: it is proven for Best-of-N; for RL (e.g. GRPO) it is cited as thought-necessary from prior empirical work.
- The empirical support is a single synthetic graph path-finding task with small GPT-2-like models; the paper makes no claim on real LMs or natural-language benchmarks in the mapped text.
- Plain SGD comes out negatively (a lower bound shows poor coverage is possible); the draft omits this.
