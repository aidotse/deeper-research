# Map: 2608.24949 - Demystifying Reinforcement Learning Post-Training of Language Models

## Method

- L20 (overview): "iterative process that shapes an LLM's output distribution using reward signals that might not be differentiable" where "model parameters are adjusted to encourage higher-reward responses" — core RL post-training algorithm
- L30 (experimental approach): "carefully controlled experiments, showing how manipulating the base model distribution, prompt distribution, and reward function all induce changes in the success rate of post-training" — experimental methodology
- L82-92 (base distribution manipulation): SFT+ artificially inflates probability of target behavior by "fine-tuning on the correct target behavior to artificially inflate the prior probability of the desired sequence" and SFT- "fine-tuned on the opposite objective: to maximize cross-entropy loss on the target behavior to suppress" it — systematic prior manipulation
- L94-108 (reward functions): "Sparse reward remains a strict binary verifier" while "dense reward proxies" use "Levenshtein distance as a proxy for dense feedback" for string generation and "Process Reward Model (PRM) with further details" for math — reward density variation

## Setup

- L84-92 (Model families): Testing OLMo 3, Qwen2-7B, Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with SFT+, Base, and SFT- variants — multiple model scales
- L90-92 (String generation task): "target quote τ = 'life is like a box of chocolates you never know what youre gonna get'" with "SFT+" training 20% on target and 80% on random quotes — controlled sequence task
- L92 (Math reasoning task): "AIME 2025 dataset (Problem 4): Find the number of ordered pairs (x,y)...such that: 12x²-xy-6y²=0" with ground truth answer 117 — mathematical reasoning benchmark
- L100-110 (Dense rewards): Levenshtein edit distance and PRM using "five key valid sub-components of the ground-truth solution" with "exponential reward curve" preventing "local minima" — dense reward structures
- L113-116 (Prompt distributions): "narrow distribution" of 100 math-only prompts and "broad distribution" from 10k WildChat or OLMo mix prompts — distribution breadth variation

## Headline results

- L146-152 (Coverage principle): "base model's probability of producing the desired behavior has a strong effect on the success of RL post-training" where "Models artificially injected with the target quote (SFT+) demonstrated quick convergence and easily maximized the sparse reward" while "models lacking coverage completely failed to learn (SFT-)" — sparse RL requires adequate base coverage
- L152-154 (Coverage failure): "Qwen2-7B (≈3.5% prior) eventually converged after a 40-step exploration plateau, smaller models with lower priors, like Qwen3-1.7B, (≈0.5% prior) failed entirely" — threshold effects in coverage
- L154 (AIME coverage): "Models injected with the correct derivation (SFT+) quickly converged (∼85% exact match), while the unmodified Base model stalled near 10%" — math task coverage bottleneck
- L176-177 (Dense rewards breakthrough): "Despite the base model possessing a marginal ≈0.5% base probability of producing τ, with post-training failing under standard sparse rewards, the dense signal successfully guided the model out of failure, pulling its final match rate to nearly 50%" — dense rewards overcome coverage
- L178 (AIME dense reward success): "introducing the dense reward radically altered the learning dynamics...the Base model's exact match rate jumped to over 90%" and even "slightly outperformed the artificially inflated SFT+ model, which plateaued around 85%" — complex generalization with dense rewards
- L186-202 (Spurious rewards depend on prompt distribution): "narrow distribution replicates the original results, showing that the capabilities of Qwen models can actually increase with random rewards" but "when switching to a broad prompt distribution, we see minimal improvements on the MATH benchmark, and degraded performance on AMC" — distribution modulation of spurious reward effect

## Limitations

- L216-220 (Computation and limited scope): "All experiments were run on either NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU or NVIDIA L40s 48GB" limiting thoroughness, and "LLMs were used in the development of the code base/experiments, specifically modifying behavior to adapt to resource limitations" — resource constraints affect experimental breadth
- L227 (Spurious rewards limitation): "This suggests that spurious rewards improve performance only when the prompt distribution is narrow and the base model is already strongly biased toward the target domain, a restricted setting that does not apply to most of RL post-training" — spurious reward effect limited to narrow cases
- L206 (Unclear real-world applicability): Experiments use "simplified sequence-generation sandbox" which may not generalize to "full-scale LLM post-training" — controlled setting may not scale

## Assumptions and conditions

- L26 (KL-constraint assumption): "Because of the KL-constraint, post-training of LLMs can be understood as redistributing probability mass within the pre-trained distribution" — assumes KL constraint prevents learning radically new behaviors
- L41 (Coverage principle framing): "behaviors with negligible initial probability are difficult to discover and reinforce" linking "to the classical concept of exploration in RL" — assumes exploration from base distribution rather than random initialization
- L114-115 (Prompt distribution effect): "for any prompt, highly probable responses under the base model are sampled more frequently and thus disproportionately rewarded by chance" with "narrow distribution" reinforcing existing patterns — assumes base probability determines sampling frequency
- L201-202 (Specificity of harm/benefit): "Qwen's gains under a narrow D fit this explanation: the same targeted sharpening that harms GSM8K performance in OLMo 3 instead reinforces the correct math reasoning paths" — assumes reward effects are task-domain-specific

## Dimensions

- **evidence**: L148-152 (empirical): "to test this empirically, we manipulated base model priors through SFT+ and SFT- phases" showing "base model's probability of producing the desired behavior has a strong effect" — controlled experimental manipulation of base probability
- **measures**: L115-116 (capability): "tracking the log-likelihood and average per-token entropy of both in-distribution and held-out prompts throughout training, alongside a suite of reasoning benchmarks" — measures task performance and model entropy distribution
- **elicitation**: L82-92, L30 (elicited): "RL post-training" where "model parameters are adjusted to encourage higher-reward responses" during training on downstream tasks — explicitly optimizing model behavior via reward signals
- **pressure**: L80, L100-108 (training-incidental): "RL post-training algorithm" with varying reward signals (sparse vs dense) during GRPO training, not designed to pressure against anything specific — training pressure from reward optimization
