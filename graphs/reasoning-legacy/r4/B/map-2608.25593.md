# Map for 2608.25593: JIT-Agent - Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution

## Method

- L15-17 (Motivation): "Agent capability is not determined by the model alone. The agent harness, encompassing memory management, planning strategy, action protocol, and tool/skill orchestration, can dominate the contribution of the underlying foundation model." — Core premise that harness design is a first-order determinant of agent capability
- L23-26 (JIT paradigm): "We instead turn to a Just-in-Time (JIT) view of harness construction. Different tasks plainly require different harness priors." — Shifts from ahead-of-time to just-in-time harness generation
- L57-75 (Modular factorization): "An agent harness admits $\mathbf{h} = (\mathbf{M}, \mathbf{P}, \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{F})$ where $\mathbf{M}, \mathbf{P}, \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{F}$ denote memory, planning, action, and capability-orchestration modules" — Four-module protocol for executable harness representation
- L29-34 (Three-stage training): "JIT-Agent realizes harness intelligence through a three-stage training recipe: Stage I teaches task-conditioned customization, Stage II converts failed generations into bounded repair trajectories, Stage III introduces Evolutionary Group-Decoupled Policy Optimization" — Three-stage training pipeline for adaptivity, reliability, and evolvability
- L120-127 (Data curation): "For each task, $\mathcal{E}_\tau$ contains three scaffolds sampled from the task-type-matched subset $\mathcal{B}_0^{(d(\boldsymbol{\tau}))}$" — Provides task-type-relevant context during generation

## Setup

- L55-75 (Protocol definition): "The fixed protocol $\boldsymbol{\Pi}$ specifies module schemas and interfaces, their lifecycle, validation rules, and the shared execution semantics" — Explicit protocol constrains generation space
- L77-108 (Seed bank): "HarnessFactory re-implements 13 representative scaffolds spanning memory, planning, aggregation, hierarchical control, and recursive execution" — 13 hand-written reference harnesses covering major design patterns
- L127-133 (Preference function): "A preference is retained only when reward improves, neither efficiency axis degrades, and at least one efficiency gain is strict" — Multi-objective evaluation combining reward, latency, and cost
- L136-143 (Repair training): "At repair round $k$, the teacher proposes a structured revision $\Delta^{(k+1)}$ from patch space $\mathcal{P}$; retention is bounded to trajectories that become executable within two rounds" — Limits repair supervision to recoverable failures
- L150-165 (Evo-GDPO): "Evo-GDPO normalizes the three channels independently within each candidate group before aggregation, preventing their scales from overwhelming one another" — Decoupled reward, latency, and cost normalization

## Headline results

- L225-228 (Main results): "The average score rises from 74.1 to 81.8 on GLM-5.2 (+7.7) and from 66.7 to 75.5 on DeepSeek-V4-Flash (+8.8)" — Consistent improvements across backbone models
- L228-229 (Largest gains): "The largest gains appear on long-horizon planning, with +24.8 on DeepPlanning-Shopping for DeepSeek-V4-Flash and +20.2 on DeepPlanning-Travel for GLM-5.2" — Task-dependent performance gains
- L229 (Comparative advantage): "JIT-Agent + GLM-5.2 ranks first on seven benchmarks, including 93.9 on DeepSearchQA, 69.9 on AgentIF, and 93.3 on PinchBench" — Exceeds frontier models on multiple benchmarks
- L249-262 (Efficiency gains): "JIT-Agent achieves the best performance in four of six settings and has the lowest token consumption and cost in all six settings, reducing per-case cost by 14.9–54.1% relative to the cheapest fixed alternative" — Superior efficiency-performance tradeoffs
- L275 (Generalization): "JIT-Agent wins all 24 matched comparisons by 7.6 points on average, with family-level gains of 10.2, 4.0, and 8.6 points respectively" — Transfers across model families and scales

## Limitations

- L169 (Controlled comparison scope): "Streaming inference mode is designed to carry useful experience forward across a sequence of tasks" but evaluation focuses on static inference — Limited evidence on streaming harness evolution beyond laboratory conditions
- L331 (Streaming gains): "Streaming JIT finishes with higher cumulative accuracy on all three benchmarks, while API-cost and tool-use trajectories remain task-dependent and of broadly similar scale" — Streaming improvements emerge gradually from accumulated feedback
- L336-374 (Visualized examples): "The examples span bounded recursive research, constraint-heavy shopping and travel, phased web production, clue-driven research, evidence-sensitive retrieval, numerical analysis, and workspace manipulation" — Primarily tested on narrow benchmark domains

## Assumptions and conditions

- L57-75 (Protocol compliance): "All modules operate against the same frozen $\pi_\psi$, whose common runtime dependence is suppressed below" — Assumes fixed executor and common kernel
- L118-124 (Supervised signal): "Training tasks come from existing agentic benchmarks and environments, augmented with synthesized tasks" — Relies on curated training task distribution
- L127-133 (Multi-objective scalarization): "A preference is retained only when reward improves, neither efficiency axis degrades, and at least one efficiency gain is strict" — Specific Pareto domination criterion for valid preferences
- L150-165 (Online evolution policy): "A candidate is retained only if it matches or exceeds the current reward frontier and then strictly improves at least one frontier dimension" — Conservative retention policy for archive updates

## Dimensions

**Evidence**: L225-229 reports measured empirical results across nine benchmarks; results validated through repeated rollouts with reported standard deviations — "empirical" measurements on diverse agent benchmarks

**Measures**: L127-133 evaluates three performance channels: task reward (primary) and efficiency (latency, cost); L145-147 measures protocol-compliance and execution validity — "monitorability" through automated validation and cost metrics

**Elicitation**: L122-127 trains on teacher-generated examples followed by DPO with preference filtering; L136-143 converts failed generations into supervised repair trajectories; L150-165 optimizes for harnesses that improve upon archive frontier — "elicited" through multi-stage supervised learning and online policy optimization

**Pressure**: L122-127 uses SFT on teacher examples then DPO on preferences; L136-143 applies bounded repair under execution feedback; L150-165 uses clipped PPO with KL penalty during Evo-GDPO — "monitor-in-reward" through multi-stage training objectives and "training-incidental" constraints from protocol validation
