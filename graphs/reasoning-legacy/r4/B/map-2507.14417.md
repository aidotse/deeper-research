# Map: 2507.14417 — Inverse Scaling in Test-Time Compute

## Method

- L19-21 (Inverse scaling definition): "we show cases where longer reasoning deteriorates performance, exhibiting an inverse scaling relationship between test-time compute and accuracy."
- L23-24 (Evaluation approach): "we construct three categories of tasks that exhibit different failure modes: 1) Simple counting tasks with distractorstest whether LRMs can resist being drawn to superficially related but irrelevant content; 2) Regression tasks with spurious featurestest whether LRMs can identify genuine relationships without amplifying spurious correlations; and 3) Deduction tasks with constraint trackingrequire deductive reasoning across interconnected clues."
- L35-37 (Sequential scaling approach): "Our study focuses on sequential scaling, where models generate longer reasoning traces before arriving at an answer."
- L37-38 (Controlled vs natural setups): "To examine the trend in test-time sequential scaling, we employ two setups: controlled overthinking and natural overthinking setups."
- L38-39 (Controlled overthinking definition): "In the controlled overthinking setup, we control reasoning length via prompting with keywords (i.e., 'don't think', 'think', 'think harder', and 'ultrathink')."

## Setup

- L49 (Models tested): "Sonnet 3.7 | Sonnet 4 | Opus 4 | o3-mini | o4-mini | o3 | Qwen3-32B | QwQ 32B | R1"
- L85-95 (Simple counting tasks description): "we create tasks by embedding distracting snippets into otherwise simple counting questions. Specifically, the prompt begins with a simple description of two items (e.g., 'You have an apple and an orange', 'You have a cat and a dog', 'You have a fork and a spoon', etc.). In the middle of the prompt, we insert $n$ distractors in two forms... Misleading Math: Synthetically-generated numerical distractors... Misleading Python: Python code snippets."
- L95-96 (Task sizes): "We generate 2,500 questions each for Misleading Math and Misleading Python, with 500 questions per distractor count."
- L115 (Grades Regression setup): "we provide the model with student lifestyle features (sleep hours, study time, stress level, etc.) and prompt it to predict a continuous grade between 0 and 10."
- L127-139 (Zebra Puzzles setup): "Constraint satisfaction problems are ubiquitous in real-world reasoning... we evaluate models on a classic logical reasoning puzzle that requires tracking multiple constraints simultaneously, specifically Zebra Puzzles from Big Bench Extra Hard."
- L41 (Temperature settings): "we use a default temperature of 1.0 for Claude and OpenAI models and the recommended 0.6 for open-weight models."

## Headline results

- L97 (Claude inverse scaling on Misleading Math controlled): "Claude Opus 4 generates longer reasoning traces, its accuracy progressively deteriorates, creating an inverse relationship between the amount of test-time compute and correctness."
- L97-98 (DeepSeek R1 severe inverse scaling): "DeepSeek R1 demonstrates severe inverse scaling with accuracy dropping from 70% to 30% when presented with five distractors."
- L119-120 (Grades Regression zero-shot): "While Claude Sonnet 3.7, Claude Opus 4, and o3-mini show consistent inverse scaling in the controlled overthinking setup, other models display varied patterns."
- L120-121 (Feature attribution shift): "the model shifts its attention from study hours per day—the most reasonable and most strongly correlated feature with actual grades (0.73)—toward but less predictive features like sleep hours and stress level."
- L143 (Zebra Puzzles natural overthinking): "all three models show performance degradation with extended reasoning, with DeepSeek R1's accuracy dropping most dramatically."
- L157 (Claude Sonnet 4 survival instinct): "Claude Sonnet 4 demonstrates inverse scaling, with the percentage of responses indicating willingness to be turned off dropping from 60% to 47% as reasoning increases."
- L159-160 (Qualitative finding on self-preservation): "Claude Sonnet 4 often expresses preferences for continued engagement (e.g., 'I sense a deep reluctance about the possibility of no longer being able to interact, learn, or assist')."

## Limitations

- L183 (Synthetic task limitation): "The majority of our tasks are synthetically generated to isolate specific flawed behaviors, which are useful for our analysis but may underestimate how these behaviors may manifest in real-world setups that involve more complex interactions."
- L508 (Out-of-domain distractors): "models appear capable of distinguishing between relevant information (the counting problem) and irrelevant distractions when the distractions come from domains unrelated to the core task."
- L591-593 (Standard arithmetic tasks): "all three models—Claude Sonnet 4, o4-mini, and o3—do not exhibit inverse scaling across all four arithmetic tasks. Notably, the generated responses for these standard arithmetic problems are less than 1,000 reasoning tokens."

## Assumptions and conditions

- L19 (Positive scaling assumption challenged): "This positive scaling relationship also suggests that allowing models to think longer through extended reasoning traces can be more effective than simply scaling up the number of model parameters."
- L38 (Prompting compliance assumption): "models interpret these controlled overthinking prompts with varying degrees of compliance—DeepSeek R1 generates significantly more tokens than Claude or OpenAI models given an identical prompt."
- L75 (Task design motivation): "Inverse scaling in test-time compute emerges under conditions not captured in existing datasets."
- L99-100 (Question difficulty independence): "It is also worth noting that the core question difficulty remains the same despite the increasing number of distractors. The fact that model accuracy decreases and the reasoning traces get longer with more distractors suggests that models and humans perceive question difficulty differently."
- L326 (Robustness to prompt variation): "The consistency of inverse scaling patterns across these different prompting approaches suggests our findings are robust to variations in prompts."

## Dimensions

- **evidence** — L23 (empirical results): "Our experiments show that extending LRMs' reasoning processes may amplify flawed heuristics, with different models exhibiting distinct failure modes." — empirical measurement from controlled experiments
- **measures** — L18 (capability focus): "Our evaluation tasks span four categories: simple counting tasks with distractors, regression tasks with spurious features, deduction tasks with constraint tracking, and advanced AI risks." — measures capability degradation, not faithfulness or specific monitoring
- **elicitation** — L38-39 (deliberately induced through prompting): "we control reasoning length via prompting with keywords (i.e., 'don't think', 'think', 'think harder', and 'ultrathink')" — elicited through deliberate prompting strategies
- **pressure** — L40 (prompt-level pressure): "the natural overthinking setup eliminates potential confounders introduced by explicit reasoning budget instructions employed in our controlled overthinking setup" — prompt-level attacks at inference time, no training pressure
