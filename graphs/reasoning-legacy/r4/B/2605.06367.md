## Limitations and conditions

- "our presentation focused on binary Gaussian mixtures with isotropic covariances, even though the framework naturally extends to multi-class and non-isotropic settings" (Sec. 5, l.261) — scope of the derived results: C = 2, isotropic.
- "it is fundamentally restricted to capturing first- and second-order structures" (Sec. 5, l.261) — the theory sees only class means and variances; outliers and intra-class multimodality are outside it.
- "real data classes often have internal multimodal structure that cannot be reduced to a single Gaussian component" (Sec. 5, l.261) — an assumption the data model rests on, which real data violates.
- "in the high-dimensional proportional limit where $N,P,M\rightarrow\infty$ with fixed $\mathcal{O}(1)$ ratios" (l.88) — the results are asymptotic; finite-size simulations are shown to converge toward them.
- "Unless stated otherwise, we assume $\forall\ c,\ ||\bm{m}_{c}||=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$ and $\sigma_{c}=\mathcal{O}(1)$ ." (l.70) — scaling assumptions behind Result 1. The authors note that a subextensive centroid produces the same delay as strong imbalance (l.136).
- "For clarity, we restrict our focus to a binary Gaussian mixture ( $C=2$ ) evaluated at small diffusion times ( $t\ll 1$ )." (l.148) — Results 2 and 3 hold in the small-diffusion-time regime only.
- "Fig.3 shows the test error under three structural ablations relative to a balanced baseline with orthogonal, equal-norm centroids" (l.181) — the hierarchy in Result 2 is read off three ablations at specific parameter values, not derived as a general ordering.
- "it is unlikely to be observed empirically: finite learning rates effectively bypass the associated small-eigenvalue directions" (l.162) — the final memorisation timescale is a theoretical object, not something practice will see.
- "While first and second-order moments do not fully resolve the complexity of memorization—as evidenced by residual discrepancies" (l.255) — the RF-to-DDPM agreement is qualitative, and the two models operate on "distinct gap scales" (l.245).

## Other findings

- The paper has no self-training, recursive or synthetic-data loop. The models are trained once on fixed data. The draft is right that the paper fits block J at most as an adjacent tool, but that judgement belongs in `selected because` or a note, not in `result`.
- The DDPM validation tests memorisation gaps only. Generalisation order and speciation times are not tested on real data in the mapped text.
- The practical suggestion, reweighting toward lower-signal classes, is explicitly untested: "We leave a systematic study of such interventions to future work." (l.259)
- The abstract's "delayed" speciation and the intro's "earlier diffusion times" (l.31) describe the same thing from two directions. The minority class appears later in the backward (generation) process, which is a smaller forward diffusion time t.
