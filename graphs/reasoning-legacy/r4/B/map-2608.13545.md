# Map: 2608.13545 — LittleLearner: Language Models Under Pedagogically Controlled Knowledge Exposure

## Method

- L9 (Curriculum construction): "a curated 88B-token pretraining corpus tailored to U.S. elementary school material, explicitly excluding concepts, facts, and vocabulary taught above Grade 5."
- L35 (Approach): "we instantiate this approach with LittleLearner, a 5B-parameter language model trained from scratch under controlled knowledge exposure."
- L75-76 (Multi-stage filtering): "We divide the web corpus FineWeb-Edu into an elementary school (K–5) and Beyond-K–5 subset, via multi-stage filtering that enforces constraints on knowledge, syntactic complexity, and reasoning."
- L78-80 (Age-of-Acquisition filter): "We begin with a lightweight rule-based filter to enforce coarse age alignment. Among several proxies for linguistic and conceptual difficulty, Age-of-Acquisition (AoA) provides the strongest separation between grade levels."
- L87-88 (Curriculum-based classification): "Our filtering pipeline is centered on classifiers trained on LLMJ-annotated data. We first apply a lightweight FastText classifier. For the ~266M samples whose grade band matches the AoA assignment, we apply a stronger, 50× more expensive ModernBERT classifier."
- L91-93 (Symbolic filtering): "Due to the low frequency of formulas and the difficulty in learning reliable signals from LLMJ annotations, we apply a rule-based symbolic filtering step. Each document is scanned using a fixed set of regular expressions covering broad notation families."
- L97 (Frequency sampling): "The post-filter corpus is larger than our training target, allowing the final sampling stage to further tighten the exposure boundary rather than subsample uniformly."

## Setup

- L107-108 (Training): "we train a 5B-parameter language model from scratch, which we call LittleLearner. We follow the Qwen3 architecture and pretrain the model for 100 hours on 8 NVIDIA B200 GPUs."
- L111 (Baseline comparison): "We compare against a model of the same size and type trained on an unfiltered version of FineWeb-Edu (Unfiltered) and Gemma 2B as an external model of similar size, but trained on 2T instead of 80B tokens."
- L115-117 (Linguistic complexity validation): "In Figure 4 we evaluate linguistic complexity using the CLEAR dataset, which provides Bradley–Terry easiness scores for text passages derived from expert teacher judgments."
- L133 (Mathematical complexity): "We validate a stronger mathematical familiarity on K–5 and a limited exposure on Beyond-K–5 on CoMTA."
- L139 (Factual knowledge evaluation): "We validate LittleLearner's ability to retrieve factual knowledge within and beyond its training scope on Jeopardy science questions."
- L155 (Mathematical reasoning evaluation): "We characterize the mathematical reasoning boundary of LittleLearner using MathCAMPS, a dataset of synthetic questions aligned with individual Common Core State Standards."

## Headline results

- L145 (Factual knowledge boundary): "LittleLearner shows a sudden drop in factual knowledge beyond its exposure horizon, while Unfiltered performs comparably across both bins."
- L155-156 (Math reasoning degradation): "Compared with Unfiltered and Gemma 2B, LittleLearner exhibits a disproportionately large decline in performance at higher grade levels."
- L175 (Scaling within bounds): "Inside the exposure range scaling delivers substantial gains, and at the smallest size LittleLearner actually outperforms the Unfiltered control."
- L175-176 (Scaling ineffective beyond boundary): "Scaling has a partial effect at the boundary of the training exposure (Grades 6 and 7), whose problem structure still overlaps with K–5 arithmetic, but essentially no effect on Grade 8, whose material lies fully outside LittleCurriculum."
- L179-180 (Post-training limitation): "Both post-training on K–5 content as well as a matched unfiltered post-training baseline are unable to recover Beyond-K–5 performance for LittleLearner."
- L193 (In-context learning limitation): "We do not find that for the 5B LittleLearner and the few shot and explanation setups we test, in-context learning (ICL) is able to unlock new reasoning capabilities."
- L203 (Pretraining dominance): "each lever amplifies LittleLearner's capabilities within its K–5 exposure, but provides limited gains in Beyond-K–5 on capabilities not well supported by pretraining."
- L204 (Pretraining as ceiling): "it is the pretraining filter, rather than the intervention, that sets the effective capability ceiling in our tested settings."

## Limitations

- L229 (Scale limitation): "Although the 5B scale is small enough for academic feasibility yet large enough for coherent production, we acknowledge that certain emergent behaviors like in-context learning may be less pronounced than at frontier scales."
- L461-462 (Capability ceiling not artifact): "If LittleLearner carried any latent Beyond-K–5 capability that a larger sample budget could surface, we are confident that it would have surfaced. The Grade-8 plateau is therefore best read as a capability ceiling."
- L442-444 (Learning path mismatch): "LittleLearner and Unfiltered do not follow the human curriculum. For example, it performs better on division by a multi-digit divisor than on division by a single-digit divisor, despite single-digit division being a prerequisite."
- L563-566 (Hallucination pattern): "on out-of-scope prompts, the model does not express uncertainty but instead generates structured yet incorrect explanations. This behavior suggests that the model lacks the underlying concepts, but nevertheless attempts to explain them."

## Assumptions and conditions

- L29-30 (Knowledge boundary assumption): "When prior exposure is unknown, it is difficult to determine whether methods like in-context learning or post-training lead to genuine capability growth or merely elicit knowledge or skills already present in the training data."
- L33-34 (Pedagogical grounding): "we focus on developmental boundaries that more directly constrain exposure, as age-level curricula provide a pedagogically grounded proxy for the concepts, language, and reasoning demands available at a given developmental stage."
- L60-61 (Pretraining dominance): "A growing body of work shows that the pretraining distribution is the primary determinant of generalization and downstream capability, with architecture and optimization playing comparatively minor roles."
- L100 (Precision-first design): "LittleCurriculum is intended as a sharply-bounded corpus for controlled exposure experiments rather than an exhaustive sample of elementary school material."
- L102 (Retention validation): "The pipeline successfully reduces the retention of Beyond-K–5 content to 0% while preserving ~35% of K–5."

## Dimensions

- **evidence** — L35 (empirical via controlled setup): "a 5B-parameter language model trained from scratch under controlled knowledge exposure" allows "direct study of whether capabilities can be extended beyond a knowledge boundary or remain tied to pretraining exposure" — empirical measurement under controlled pretraining conditions
- **measures** — L139-155 (capability measurement): "We validate LittleLearner's ability to retrieve factual knowledge within and beyond its training scope on Jeopardy science questions" and "We characterize the mathematical reasoning boundary of LittleLearner using MathCAMPS" — measuring standard downstream task performance, not faithfulness or specific monitoring
- **elicitation** — L205 (observational): "We view this not as a negative result but as a starting point: the same controlled exposure that exposes the limits of these standard interventions also makes LittleLearner (and LittleCurriculum) a useful sandbox for studying algorithms" — studying natural capabilities from restricted pretraining without deliberate elicitation
- **pressure** — L203-204 (observational, none): "each lever amplifies LittleLearner's capabilities within its K–5 exposure, but provides limited gains in Beyond-K–5 on capabilities not well supported by pretraining" — testing standard interventions (scaling, post-training, ICL) with no special pressure mechanisms
