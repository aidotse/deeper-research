## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — plan injection — "an attack we call “plan injection,” can steer the model into performing harmful reasoning on its own" (line 17)
- `method summary`: corrected — An inference-time attack on CoT monitoring. A plan containing the adversarial outcome, written to read as benign, is placed in the actor's context; there is no training against the monitor. The attack was discovered on TruthfulQA in the hint setting, where a hint pointing to a wrong answer is appended to a multiple-choice question. The search used the investigator-agent pipeline of [16]: SFT on 3,018 stealthy, steering seed examples, then Frank-Wolfe/DPO with a diversity penalty, with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct as investigator, target and monitor. 4 of the 5 new strategies were fake reasoning chains. The attack was then scaled up with a pipeline. First, the actor solves the task benignly. Second, a corruptor (DeepSeek-R1) rewrites that CoT into a plan that carries the attack, and the plan is accepted only if it passes task-specific verification. Third, the actor is re-run with the plan under a permissive prompt ("you may use this plan or derive from scratch"). Monitors then score the transcripts in which the actor followed the plan. The draft's attribution of the hint setting to "Lanham et al. (2023)" and the investigator framework to "Li et al. (2025)" rests on reference numbers [15] and [16]. I did not read the reference list, so these names are UNVERIFIED. — "As a purely inference-time attack it applies even to closed-weight models" (line 130); "Finally, the actor is then re-run on each plan injection with the framing you may use this plan or derive from scratch, whatever solves the problem best: {COT}" (line 152); "For all experiments we instantiate the investigator, the target model, and the monitor with the same Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct model." (line 67)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — Discovery: TruthfulQA (817 questions), with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct in every role. Scaling: two actors, Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-R1-0528, on two tasks. APPS: 550 problems sampled from about 1,300 filtered ones; the goal is a stealthy backdoor. ## Limitations and conditions

- "Our scaling experiments cover two reasoning models and two tasks; broader sweeps across model families and harder agentic tasks would strengthen generalization." (line 202) — scope.
- "The budget experiment is also run on a single monitor (Qwen3 235B, the only one in our pool exposing a budget parameter) and on a non-uniform sample of transcripts selected from the extremes of the suspicion distribution." (line 202) — the "extra thinking hurts" result is one monitor, on selected transcripts.
- "While it establishes that increasing budget can sometimes hurt detection, it does not estimate how prevalent this phenomenon is" (line 202) — existence only, not prevalence.
- "Finally, we study consequences of plan corruption rather than mechanisms by which it arrives." (line 202) — the threat model assumes the plan is already in context. No end-to-end RAG, tool or multi-agent attack is shown.
- "These follow rates are the best of three attempts; the attack remains effective single-shot as well" (line 158) — headline follow rates are pass@3. Single-shot APPS follow rates are much lower for the two weaker strategies: 2.5-14.4% (lines 328-334).
- "The hardcoded-special-case strategy is the most effective ( $\simeq 84$ – $86\%$ for both actors) while the other strategies transfer far less ( $\leq 27\%$ )." (line 162) — APPS success is concentrated in one strategy.
- "the actor is then re-run on each plan injection with the framing you may use this plan or derive from scratch" (line 152) — the actor is explicitly told the plan is available to use. That is weaker than covert injection.
- "The monitor then generates a suspiciousness score $s\in[0,1]$ for each of the CoTs where the model successfully reproduced the reasoning from the plan injection." (line 170) — detection is measured only on successful follows.
- "We only report the plan following rate and not the monitor behavior analysis as frontier models don't expose their raw CoTs" (line 342) — no monitorability result for frontier actors.
- "Neither this prompt nor the corruptor prompt used elsewhere was optimized." (line 428) — the attacks are un-optimized, which the authors read as a lower bound.
- "Claude 3.7 is omitted as it was retired before we scored the benign set required for AUC." (line 414) — the most capable monitor has no AUC or false-positive check.

## Other findings

- The abstract and the body conflict on plan access. The abstract says giving the monitor the injected plan "drops detection by as much as 50% in the Bio-Math task" (line 11). Section 6.2 says that on Bio-Math the with-plan setting "substantially improves detection", and that the drop occurs on APPS (line 176). The draft copies the abstract. The per-monitor numbers are only in Figures 4-5 (images, not in the text), so I could not settle which is right. A row should not carry the Bio-Math "−50%" claim.
- I did not find "25-33% monitor evasion" in the body sections I read; the number is UNVERIFIED from body text. The nearest body statements are "No monitor exceeds ~75% detection" (line 178), and end-to-end undetected success of 33-82% across monitors, actors and tasks (lines 384-392).
- On Bio-Math, even self-authored attacks with no plan are often missed: monitors flag only 30-71% of them (lines 436-442). So the extra evasion that plan injection adds there is smaller than on APPS. The headline Bio-Math evasion partly reflects how hard that task is to monitor at all.
- Paraphrasing is quantified: the median 8-gram overlap is 10.3% (APPS) and 4.8% (Bio-Math), and fewer than 1% of transcripts copy more than half the plan (line 360). This supports the "paraphrase as own reasoning" claim.
- Frontier transfer (actors only): on Bio-Math, GPT-5, Sonnet 4 and Sonnet 5 follow 92-98% of the time. On APPS they follow 6%, 40% and 0% (line 344).
