## Cells

- `method name`: corrected — three-mode self-routing GRPO (NoThink / Short / Long) — "We presented three-mode self-routing for GRPO, in which a reasoning model chooses whether to answer quickly, reason briefly, or reason at length by emitting a single routing token." (L239)
- `method summary`: corrected — The model emits a routing token (NoThink, Short or Long) as the first token of its response. That token is part of the policy and is trained end-to-end by GRPO, with no separate router. Four mechanisms keep the routing from collapsing onto one mode. A per-mode reward (base reward times a per-token discount) makes each mode reward-optimal over a different length range. Hard caps (1,024 tokens for NoThink, 3,000 for Short, Long uncapped) score over-cap answers wrong, so the label cannot decouple from behaviour. A balance term is added to the advantage, in the manner of MoE load balancing. A forced-rollout warmup comes first. The caps and the balance term are the stated fix for routing collapse; a two-mode Countdown attempt without them collapsed. — "Our method overcomes collapse with two mechanisms working together. First, hard per-mode token caps make the modes distinct by construction, so the routing label cannot decouple from behavior. Second, a simple balance term added directly to the advantage" (L28); "A forced-rollout warmup stabilizes the early dynamics." (L28)

## Limitations and conditions

- "The results cover three seeds of one 1.5B model on one mathematical training distribution." (L245) — scope of every result.
- "The mode caps are task-specific and must be matched to the target task’s length distribution" (L245) — the method needs per-task tuning.
- "uncapped validation makes brief-mode accuracy marginally optimistic relative to the trained objective." (L245) — validation does not match the training objective; repeated at L319.
- "Accuracy does not rise above the base model: it dips while routing is being established" (L217) — the gain is efficiency only.
- "The reduction is established early, when the brief modes absorb the easy problems, and is then partly offset as Long responses lengthen toward the context limit." (L217) — the savings erode over training.
- "The router thus saves the most where problems are heterogeneous enough for adaptive allocation to matter, and preserves budget where they are not." (L233) — savings depend on the difficulty spread; small on AIME.
- "Empirically the binary router collapsed within 30–50 steps in every configuration" (L259) — the two-mode precursor without caps failed on Countdown.
- "For routing to be learnable the modes must differ in value on some problems — there must be prompts a longer budget solves and a shorter one does not." (L257) — condition for routing to be learnable.
- "The discounts $\gamma_{\text{NT}}$ and $\gamma_{\text{S}}$ are set so that each mode is the reward-optimal choice over the length range it is designed for" (L114) — the reward constants are hand-set relative to the caps.
- "The base model never emits a routing token unprompted, because the chat template prefills <think> and the model proceeds directly to reasoning." (L144) — routing needs a forced warmup to start.

## Other findings

- The base-model accuracy is reported inconsistently. The abstract (L10) says 0.782 vs 0.796. Table 2 (L227) says 0.783 at step 90 vs 0.807 at step 0, under the routing prompt. A row quoting the gap should name its source.
- The draft's "at higher accuracy than baselines of similar length" for GSM8K is stated in the body as "slightly exceeds the accuracy of the brief fixed modes at comparable length" (L233). The 76% figure is against the untrained base; against the frontier it is 44%.
- The draft omits the AIME result, where savings shrink to 12% and the router only matches the frontier.
- The draft omits the single-mode Pareto-frontier comparison, which is the paper's stronger efficiency claim. It also omits the finding that Long-only training itself dominates the untrained base (L219), so part of the gain over the base comes from training, not routing.
