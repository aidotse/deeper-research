- `method name`: confirmed — DATPO (Difficulty-Adaptive Sentence-entropy-guided Tree-structured Policy Optimization) — "we propose DATPO (Difficulty-Adaptive Sentence-entropy-guided Tree-structured Policy Optimization)" (line 25)
- `method summary`: corrected — An RLVR training method that changes the structure of train-time rollouts rather than the objective. It builds a two-phase tree: N base rollouts, whose mean reward estimates the prompt's difficulty, then forking at the starts of the highest-entropy sentences, with the number of forks and branches scaled by (1 − difficulty). Advantages are block-level Monte Carlo values. A sibling-diversity bonus (cosine distance of embeddings, from an external embedding model) is added only to blocks with positive base advantage, and its weight is annealed to 0 over training. The design rests on a preliminary GRPO study in which larger rollout budgets lowered pass@256 when training on easy problems and raised it on hard ones. — "we apply this bonus exclusively to blocks with positive base advantages to avoid incentivizing the exploration of incorrect paths" (line 149); "we scale the tree expansion proportionally to this difficulty, where a lower $V(\mathrm{root})$ indicates a more challenging problem" (line 125); "The diversity coefficient $\alpha$ is initialized to 0.2 and linearly annealed to 0 over the course of training" (line 941)

## Limitations and conditions

- "calculating the sibling-diversity term requires additional forward passes through an external embedding model, which introduces computational overhead" (line 303) — cost of the method.
- "Deriving both the empirical difficulty and Monte Carlo state values from a limited number of rollouts (e.g., $N=4,B=4$ ) can inject noise into the estimations, occasionally yielding high variance during policy updates" (line 305) — the difficulty estimate and the credit assignment are noisy.
- "resource limitations restricted our empirical validation to the 3B-4B parameter regime, leaving DATPO's scalability to larger models ( $\geq 7$ B) unverified" (line 307) — scope: two small base models only.
- "as our evaluation primarily focuses on mathematical reasoning, the framework's direct effectiveness in other rigorous domains, such as complex logical reasoning or code generation, requires further investigation" (line 307) — scope: math only.
- "We intentionally employ a larger maximum branch count ( $B_{\max}=4$ ) compared to the baselines" (line 941) — DATPO's tree shape differs from the baselines'. The comparison is matched on total generated tokens per problem, not on tree shape: "we maintain a comparable total number of generated tokens per problem across all methods" (line 213).
- "applying $k=8$ for MATH500 and $k=64$ for the remaining datasets" (line 233) — the coverage and test-time scaling results hold at small to moderate k only. Larger k is not reported in the main results.
- "For robustness, the pass@ $k$ results are computed by averaging over three independent evaluation runs" (line 207) — three evaluation runs. Training seeds and variance across them are not reported in the mapped places.
- "This stability can be attributed to the annealed sibling-diversity term, which injects semantic diversity early in training to prevent premature convergence" (line 225) — a causal attribution offered without an ablation in the mapped places.

## Other findings

- AttnRL is called "the strongest baseline", but on Qwen3-4B-Base the strongest pass@k baseline in Table 2 is GRPO, at 58.3 (lines 185, 191). So DATPO's pass@k margin over the best baseline there is +2.1, not +3.0. On Qwen2.5-3B-Base, AttnRL is the best pass@k baseline (53.0), so the +1.9 holds.
- DATPO is not best on every benchmark. On Qwen2.5-3B, MATH500 pass@8 is 81.7 vs AttnRL's 82.1 (lines 177, 179). On Qwen3-4B, AMC23 avg@64 is 48.4 vs AttnRL's 52.5 (lines 191, 193).
- The maj@k claim is a gain of maj@k over the method's own avg@k, on one model. It is not a head-to-head maj@k comparison against the baselines (line 235).
- For cluster C, the relevant point is that DATPO's average pass@k is above the base model's at the k tested: 54.9 vs 37.3, and 60.4 vs 51.2 (lines 169, 183, 179, 193). But k is only 8 or 64. The paper does not test the large-k regime where the "RL only selects" argument it cites (Yue et al., line 19) is usually made. So the paper does not settle the create-vs-select question; it frames itself as mitigating that limitation.
- The draft's "No single headline number" is outdated: the body gives +1.9 and +3.0 pass@k, and +7.2 for maj@k.
