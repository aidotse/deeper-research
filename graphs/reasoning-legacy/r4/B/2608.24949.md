## Limitations and conditions

- "Although simplified, this setup enables precise analysis of how factors like the base model's distribution affect outcomes, an analysis that would be impossible in full-scale LLM post-training." (L30) — all results come from toy tasks (one string, one AIME problem); transfer to full-scale post-training is not tested.
- "our idealized setup examining only a single problem enables us to construct an accurate, rich, and dense reward" (L110) — the dense-reward headline (Base 92.2% on AIME) rests on a hand-built PRM for a single problem with known sub-steps; it does not show such rewards are available in practice.
- "However, as in classical RL we find that reward shaping has its limits; the SFT+ model trained with dense rewards underperforms the sparse variant" (L176) — dense shaping can hurt when coverage already exists.
- "while the unmodified Base model stalled near 10%" (L154) and "smaller models with lower priors, like Qwen3-1.7B, ( $\approx 0.5\%$ prior) failed entirely" (L152) — the coverage failure is a sparse-reward result, conditioned on low base prior.
- "This suggests that spurious rewards improve performance only when the prompt distribution is narrow and the base model is already strongly biased toward the target domain, a restricted setting that does not apply to most of RL post-training." (L202) — spurious-reward gains are conditioned on both prompt narrowness and base-model prior, not on the prompt distribution alone.
- "We exclude algorithmic variation from our scope, deferring to the extensive existing research on RL algorithmic choices" (L22) — results are not claimed across RL algorithms.
- "Because of the KL-constraint, post-training of LLMs can be understood as redistributing probability mass within the pre-trained distribution." (L26) — framing assumption behind the entropy analysis.
- "LLMs were used in the development of the code base/experiments, specifically modifying behavior to adapt to resource limitations" (L220) — stated resource limitations shaped the experiments.

## Other findings

- The draft's `result` misses the paper's main claim for cluster C: with a dense, correct reward, RL can teach behaviours with negligible base support, which the authors say contradicts the "RL only selects" position (L42, L182, L206). The paper sides against pure selection, but only under dense rewards; under sparse rewards it supports the coverage/selection view. A row filed under "create or select" needs both halves.
- The draft says entropy compares "pretraining, SFT and RL stages"; the text says pretraining, SFT and DPO (L26), and the checkpoints used are OLMo 3 Base, SFT, DPO (L116).
- Internal inconsistencies: L150 says "the unmodified Qwen3-8B Base" failed, but Qwen3-8B is not among the listed models (L84; L152 names Qwen3-1.7B). The conclusion says the spurious-reward effect "depends entirely on the post-training prompt distribution" (L206), while L202 says the base model's distribution co-determines it. Appendix B (L226) says the prompt distribution "controls the scope of degradation, not whether degradation occurs", contradicting L202's "determine both the scope of degradation, and whether degradation occurs".
- Sample sizes are small: AIME evaluation uses 128 samples (L156) on one problem; narrow-distribution training uses 100 prompts (L116).
