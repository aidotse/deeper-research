## Limitations and conditions

- "In the course of the paper we have made various simplifying assumptions. Some of these can be relaxed in a straightforward fashion, while others are more fundamental." (7.1 Simplifications in the Problem Formulation, line 434) — scope of all results.
- "Our formulation in Section 2 is a simplification (one that is closer in spirit to supervised fine-tuning)" (7.1, line 438) — prompt/response split; real pre-training does not split examples this way.
- "Much of our analysis focuses on the realizable/well-specified setting where $\pi_{\texttt{D}}\in\Pi$ ." (7.1, line 442) — realizability assumption behind the main theorem.
- "In the general misspecified setting where $\pi_{\texttt{D}}\notin\Pi$ , the coverage may instead scale with the approximation error" (Coverage can converge under severe misspecification, line 187) — the main guarantee degrades under misspecification unless the class is convex or tournaments are used.
- "Our treatment assumes the distribution over prompts $\mathcal{\mu}$ is the same for pre-training and post-training." (7.1, line 444) — no distribution shift between pre- and post-training.
- "We show that a good coverage profile is necessary for BoN to succeed on downstream tasks." (7.1, line 446) and "it is not clear what the minimal conditions required for RL are" (7.1, line 446) — the necessity/sufficiency result is for BoN; for RL it is not established.
- "We therefore posit that the data distribution covers such a downstream task, in the sense that it includes high-reward responses with some bounded-below probability." (Post-training and the coverage profile, line 92) — the link to downstream performance assumes the pre-training data itself covers the task.
- "Our results focus on coverage at the sequence level." (7.1, line 448) — answer-level coverage for CoT reasoning is weaker and not the object analysed.
- "The notion of coverage we focus on, the coverage profile, is mathematically convenient but may be conservative in regard to downstream performance" (Semantic coverage, line 466) — the measure may be conservative.
- "they are conservative from a modern generalization perspective" (4.2 Discussion, line 262) — covering-number bounds are conservative for overparameterized models.
- "The only drawback (which is fundamental) is that since the class has low capacity, the coarse-grained term dominates for most parameter regimes, and the improvement as $N$ scales is quite modest." (4.1, line 210) — the faster-in-N benefit is modest for low-dimensional linear models.
- "Thus, while $\sigma_{\star}^{2}$ can be as large as $B^{2}H$ in the worst case, we expect it to be smaller in general." (4.1, line 216) — the high-dimensional result avoids H only if the inherent variance is small; worst case it scales with H.
- "We assume $\Theta\subseteq\left\{\theta:\|\theta\|\leq 1\right\}$ is convex, and $\sup_{h,x,y_{1:h}}\|\phi(x,y_{1:h})\|\leq B$ for some $B\geq{}1$ ." (Autoregressive linear models, line 103) — bounded-norm, bounded-feature assumption for the linear-model results.
- "When $\Pi$ is small, we can implement this tournament by simply evaluating the empirical coverage in Eq. 28 for each pair." (A simple tournament for maximizing coverage, line 399) — the selection procedure is practical only for small candidate sets.
