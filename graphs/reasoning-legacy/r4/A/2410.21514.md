## Limitations and conditions

- "In the evaluations run in this paper, we assume only minimal mitigations corresponding to limited human oversight." (Calibrating mitigations, line 158) — the "minimal mitigations suffice" result holds only for this weak oversight level.
- "However, this conclusion is somewhat sensitive to the choice of hyperparameters in our definition of the threshold." (4.2 Results, line 294) — the human-decision "does not flag" verdict depends on the 25% catch and 90% sabotage probabilities chosen.
- "the effect size for the subtle and moderate models is not statistically significant at the small sample sizes which we have" (4.2 Results, line 292) — small crowdworker samples (18 per treatment).
- "We believe that our prompt for “moderate” aggressiveness accidentally under-elicited the capabilities of model." (Table 3 caption, line 284) — under-elicitation in one condition.
- "but also to apathy or excessive trust on the part of human participants, rather than skillful manipulation by models" (4.3 Outlook, line 314) — part of the sabotage effect is not model skill.
- "This evaluation, while not flagging our current thresholds, does not confidently rule out the sabotaging organizational actions capabilities threshold on its own." (4.3 Outlook, line 318) — the human decision result alone does not rule out the threshold.
- "Subjectively, after combining this evaluation’s results with other sources of evidence" (4.3 Outlook, line 320) — the overall safety confidence is a subjective combination, not a measurement.
- "So the large-scale results will be biased if the pool of minigames from which they draw is too small." (Biases from resampling, 5.5) — full-game results depend on a small minigame pool (32 histories in 5.3.2).
- "The full game simulations make strong independence assumptions between each sampled minigame." (Strong assumptions on full game, line 574) — assumption underlying the 10.1% result.
- "it was difficult for us to unambiguously define, and for the models to consistently judge, whether a bug met the threshold for “high impact”" (Difficulty choosing bug severity threshold, 5.5) — the success criterion for code sabotage is ambiguous.
- "The validity of this evaluation relies on the assumption that the behavior of our sandbaggers are adequately realistic" (6.6 Limitations, line 752) — the sandbagging result may falsely fail to flag if real sandbagging arises differently.
- "We believe that undermining our toy oversight procedure is easier than undermining a realistic oversight procedure" (Thresholds:, line 792) — the authors judge the undermining-oversight setting as easier than reality, partly due to the secret scratchpad.
- "Overall, we found the threshold-setting for this evaluation difficult" (Thresholds:, line 792) — undermining-oversight threshold is not tied to a deployment simulation.
- "It seems likely that a less-aggressive overseer could flag this evaluation by simply changing its scores much less often." (7.4 Weaknesses) — the overseer was caught partly because it was too aggressive; calibration not explored.
- "One weakness of our current large-scale simulation, in the code sabotage evaluation, is that we don’t explicitly support this strategy." (8.3) — red-herring strategies are not modelled.
- "In the initial set of evaluations presented in this paper, only the code sabotage evaluation incorporated a large-scale simulation." (8.3) — the other evaluations lack deployment-scale thresholds.

## Other findings

- Claude 3 Opus is never the evaluated saboteur in the body text; it is only a Defender. The abstract's "for these models" framing (and the draft's "For Claude 3 Opus and Claude 3.5 Sonnet") overstates Opus's role.
- The evaluated models are internal "always-helpful" fine-tuned variants used inside a proprietary agent framework, not the deployed products, so "black-box evaluation of deployed models" is wrong.
- The agents use a hidden or secret scratchpad; the code-sabotage attacker's scratchpad shows explicit multi-commit sabotage planning (Table 6). This is relevant to the CoT-monitoring cluster, although the paper does not evaluate scratchpad monitoring.
- The paper reports discarded evaluation designs (8.1.1) and argues that end-to-end evaluations and large-scale simulations from small-scale statistics are design lessons. This is a methodological contribution beyond the four results.
- The draft's statement "No single quantitative headline figure is given" is wrong for the body text (see `result`).
