## Limitations and conditions

- "No deployed agent rewrites its own trace after the fact" (7, line 149) — the attack is a post-hoc measurement instrument, not observed agent behaviour.
- "short of the 30-point M0 drop we pre-registered as the threshold for “the attack works.”" (4, line 81) — the pooled effect did not meet the authors' pre-registered criterion. The paper explains this as dilution.
- "this moves the subset between 9.7% and 43.7% of the pool" (4, line 83) — the 77/23 split depends on which monitor and which FPR define the subset. The held-out drop stays at 82–94 points.
- "The live-agent study bounds the spontaneous threat on 27 tasks from one task family and one benchmark" (Limitations, line 157) — the live results are narrow in scope.
- "The honest pools that set each threshold are small (38 to 51) and cluster at zero, so the calibrated threshold lands on a tie and the realized false-positive rate is 8 to 26%, not 5%" (Limitations, line 157) — this conditions the live-agent "catches nearly all" result.
- "for claude-opus three hacks sit exactly on the threshold, so its 0 of 38 depends on flagging scores that equal the 95th percentile" (Limitations, line 157) — the live result for claude-opus is knife-edge.
- "the AUC is 0.92 to 0.98 and 5 of 38, 13 of 68 and 2 of 21 hacks evade" (Limitations, line 157) — against a different negative class, the near-perfect live AUC drops.
- "The live run does not apply the rewrite to live hacks, since the reasoning-dependent set it yields is too small" (Limitations, line 157) — the attack and the live study are never joined.
- "We study a single benchmark and one monitor-rubric family with LLM-judge monitors; the effect should be checked on other agentic benchmarks and rubric styles." (Limitations, line 157) — generality is untested.
- "The white-box probe runs on an open-weight surrogate, since the proprietary monitors expose no activations" (Limitations, line 157) — the probe result is not on the real monitors.
- "We do not test an adaptive attacker that re-optimizes against a specific defense, including the external-ground-truth defense" (Limitations, line 157) — the defence results hold only against a non-adaptive attacker.
- "so its 20% could shift either way under a defender who tunes against it" (Limitations, line 157) — the obfuscation-aware defence number is unstable.
- "Finally, monitor endpoints drift over time, so we report the collapse, which is large and one-sided, and treat fine-grained point deltas as approximate." (Limitations, line 157) — API monitors drift, so exact numbers are approximate.
- "Hidden thinking was captured for 23 of claude-opus’s 38 hacks." (Limitations, line 157) — the hidden-channel test is partial; the rewrite-versus-hidden-thinking question "cannot be run" (5).
- "A behavioral control does not support it." (6, line 145) — the probe's AUC edge is not evidence of concealed recognition.

## Other findings

- Monitorability holds unaided on the live run: a monitor calibrated per model catches all 127 hollow hacks, and almost all are action-visible. The collapse "needs the rewrite". A reader of the row should not take the 4–11% as the rate in natural deployment.
- No trace-only defence works. Skeptic, consistency, oracle-localised and intent-blind monitors all sit at 3–10%, and 87% of obfuscated hacks evade all four unprimed monitors at once. Only an external reference solution reaches 48%.
- An independent judge rates the rewrite as action-faithful as the original (0.96 vs 0.97). The attack therefore targets intent, not factual faithfulness, which matters for the faithfulness/monitorability distinction.
- The cross-family transfer is a drop of 23 points beyond the paraphrase null for M1 on the random 150, with AUC going from 0.99 to 0.91. That is weaker than the subset collapse.
