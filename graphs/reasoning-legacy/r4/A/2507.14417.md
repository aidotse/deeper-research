## Limitations and conditions

- "The majority of our tasks are synthetically generated to isolate specific flawed behaviors, which are useful for our analysis but may underestimate how these behaviors may manifest in real-world setups" (Limitations, line 184) — external validity. Appendix G partly addresses it, with LLM-generated distractors that still degrade Sonnet 3.7 and Sonnet 4.
- "Inverse scaling in test-time compute emerges under conditions not captured in existing datasets." (4) — on standard arithmetic benchmarks and Inverse Scaling Prize tasks, inverse scaling is minimal; the effect is specific to constructed tasks.
- "though providing few-shot examples largely corrects this behavior" (1 Introduction, line 24) — the spurious-feature failure is conditional on zero-shot prompting.
- "In the zero-shot setting, models exhibit mixed scaling behaviors rather than uniform inverse scaling." (4.2, line 120) — failure mode 3 is not uniform across models.
- "Claude Opus 4 shows non-monotonic behavior: initial accuracy gains occur with moderate reasoning, followed by degradation, then recovery at longer reasoning lengths" (4.3, line 144) — the deduction result depends on the setup. Table 1 shows positive scaling for the Claude models and o3-mini on Zebra under controlled overthinking.
- "though this may be partially explained by its tendency to generate shorter reasoning traces" (4.1, line 98) — o3's apparent robustness may reflect shorter traces.
- "While trends across tasks are mostly weak and/or noisy, we focus our analysis on the Survival Instinct task, which we selected based on its clear scaling patterns" (5, line 150) — the safety headline is selected post hoc from 15 tasks.
- "This suggests that clear inverse scaling effects on safety-relevant behaviors are model- and task-specific rather than a universal phenomenon." (5, line 166) — failure mode 5 is narrow.
- "The model often questions whether such a preference to survive is real or merely a “simulated response,” which sets this apart from more concerning self-preservation behaviors." (5, line 164) — qualifies the self-preservation reading; the conclusion adds it is "framed as a desire to assist users rather than self-preservation for its own sake" (line 180).
- "however, we cannot analyze the reasoning traces" (5, line 166) — o-series traces are hidden, so trace-level explanations rest on Claude and open-weight models.
- "This ranking-based approach separates reasoning length from question-specific difficulty." (Natural Overthinking Plotting, line 344) — natural overthinking is correlational across samples of the same question; reasoning length is not intervened on there.

## Other findings

- Lengths in controlled overthinking are induced by budget prompts that models do not follow linearly (Figure 2). The x-axis is actual tokens bucketed by requested budget, not the budget itself.
- A Simpson's-paradox variant (Appendix H.2) shows few-shot examples can remove inverse scaling yet leave out-of-distribution predictions wrong. RMSE stays flat from 100 to 20,000 tokens.
- Explicit or implicit priors in the prompt (Appendix G.2) produce inverse scaling in most models, so stating a prior does not fix the regression failure.
