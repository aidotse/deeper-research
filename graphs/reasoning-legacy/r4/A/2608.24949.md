- `method summary`: corrected — The draft omits the design, so the setup is spelled out here.
  - The base model's prior on a target behaviour is manipulated by fine-tuning. SFT+ raises it; SFT- maximises cross-entropy to suppress it to near 0%.
  - There are two toy RLVR tasks: emitting one exact movie quote, and solving one AIME 2025 problem (answer 117).
  - Each task is trained with sparse binary rewards and with dense rewards. The dense reward is Levenshtein distance for the quote and an LLM-judge PRM over five solution milestones for the AIME problem.
  - Separately, random ("spurious") rewards are applied with narrow (100 math prompts) versus broad (10k prompts) prompt distributions. Entropy and log-likelihood are tracked across OLMo 3 Base, SFT and DPO checkpoints.
  - Algorithmic variation is explicitly excluded.

  — "The SFT+ model is the base model fine-tuned on the correct target behavior to artificially inflate the prior probability of the desired sequence." (4.1, line 84); "We exclude algorithmic variation from our scope" (Figure 1 caption, line 22)

- `result`: corrected — The draft misses the paper's main counter-claim. With sparse rewards, models without coverage fail: SFT- and Qwen3-8B Base stay at 0%, and Qwen3-1.7B Base reaches only 10%. A dense reward lets low-prior models learn: Qwen3-1.7B's quote match rises to about 49%, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's AIME exact match goes from 3.9% to 92.2% with dense rewards, against 10.2% with sparse. The authors read this as evidence against the "RL only selects pass@k" position. Spurious rewards help Qwen only with narrow prompts. Broad prompts raise entropy and cause global unlearning; in OLMo SFT, narrow prompts collapse GSM8K from 86% to about 32%. — "a sufficiently dense and accurate reward function can enable learning new behaviors with vanishing support in the base model, a finding that contradicts a popular position in the literature" (6 Conclusion, line 206); "Taken together, these results demonstrate that the limitations often attributed to RLVR—such as the inability to move beyond pass@k priors—are not inherent flaws of RL post-training, but rather artifacts of the exploration bottleneck caused by sparse, binary reward functions." ## Limitations and conditions

- "our idealized setup examining only a single problem enables us to construct an accurate, rich, and dense reward" (4.2, line 110) — the dense-reward "new behaviour" result rests on a hand-built PRM for one problem. The PRM encodes the known solution milestones and gives 0.6 for the known near-miss answer 118 (Appendix D.2, line 308). A practical RLVR setting would not have such a reward.
- "Although simplified, this setup enables precise analysis" (1 Introduction, line 30) — the authors describe the tasks as "toy RLVR tasks".
- "We exclude algorithmic variation from our scope" (Figure 1 caption, line 22) — the results are not tested across RL algorithms.
- "In contrast, the Base and SFT- models remain completely flat, unable to generate the target sequence a single time during exploration" (Figure 9 caption, line 366) — with exactly 0% prior, dense rewards also fail (Qwen3-8B Base and SFT- reach 0%). The "vanishing support" claim therefore applies to small but non-zero priors (0.5% and 3.9%), not to zero support.
- "The Base model gains a slight benefit from dense rewards (7.0%) but largely fails" (Figure 7 caption, line 362) — Qwen2-1.5B (0.3% prior) mostly fails even with dense rewards.
- "the SFT+ model trained with dense rewards underperforms the sparse variant in absolute string matching performance" (5.2, line 176) — reward shaping has costs.
- "spurious rewards improve performance only when the prompt distribution is narrow and the base model is already strongly biased toward the target domain" (5.3, line 202) — the condition on the spurious-reward effect.
- "Neither setting creates new capabilities." (5.3, line 202) — this applies to random rewards specifically.

## Other findings

- For cluster C, the paper is a qualified dissenter on "RL selects rather than creates". Its own claim is that the selection limit is an artefact of sparse rewards. The evidence for creation is one string and one AIME problem with an oracle-informed dense reward, and it does not work from zero support.
- "Compare pretraining, SFT and RL stages" in the draft is loose. The entropy comparison is across the OLMo 3 Base, SFT and DPO checkpoints, each then trained with random rewards. Progressive fine-tuning delays the entropy rise.
- The paper has no Limitations section. The only scope caveats are those quoted above, spread through the body text.
