## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — LittleCurriculum (corpus) / LittleLearner (model) — "Training a 5B-parameter LLM from scratch on LittleCurriculum yields LittleLearner" (Abstract, line 10)
- `method summary`: corrected — An 88B-token corpus filtered from FineWeb-Edu to K–5 (elementary-school) content by a precision-first pipeline: age-of-acquisition pre-filtering, LLM-judge-trained classifiers, curriculum-based classification, symbolic filtering and frequency sampling. On held-out ground truth it keeps 0% of Beyond-K–5 content but only about 35% of K–5. A 5B Qwen3-architecture model is trained from scratch with a custom K–5-only tokenizer. The cooldown mixture adds Gemini-rewritten K–5 math reasoning and instruction data. The sandbox is then used to test whether scale, SFT+GRPO post-training, or in-context learning lift out-of-scope (Beyond-K–5) capability, against a same-recipe model trained on unfiltered FineWeb-Edu. — "The pipeline successfully reduces the retention of Beyond-K–5 content to 0% while preserving ~35% of K–5" (3.1.6 Validation, line 102); "We compare against a model of the same size and type trained on an unfiltered version of FineWeb-Edu ( Unfiltered )" (3.3, line 112)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — LittleCurriculum (88B tokens, from FineWeb-Edu); LittleLearner at 0.6B, 1.3B and 5B (Qwen3 architecture); same-size Unfiltered control and Gemma 2B. Evaluation: MathCAMPS (by CCSS grade), Jeopardy science questions split by NGSS grade, BPB on CLEAR and CoMTA. Post-training: SFT, then GRPO on MathCAMPS-style synthetic problems and GSM8K — "we train LittleLearner on LittleCurriculum at 0.6B, 1.3B, and 5B parameters and evaluate each on MathCAMPS" (4.1, line 176)
- `metric`: corrected — MathCAMPS pass@1 (and pass@k up to 1024) by grade band (K–5 vs Beyond-K–5), Jeopardy pass@1, and bits-per-byte, compared with the Unfiltered control — "Pass@1 accuracy on MathCAMPS estimated from 1024 rollouts." (Figure 6 caption, 3.3.2)
- `result`: corrected — Out-of-scope capability stays near floor under all three levers. Scaling (0.6B→5B) helps within K–5 and partly at Grades 6–7, but has essentially no effect on Grade 8. SFT+GRPO lifts K–5 for both models, but only modestly lifts Beyond-K–5 for LittleLearner, while Unfiltered gains much more, so the gap widens; GRPO on K–5 vs unfiltered data makes no difference. Few-shot ICL gives modest K–5 gains and none Beyond-K–5; explanations have no effect. At pass@1024 on Grade 8, LittleLearner retains only part of Unfiltered's competence (the paper gives this as fewer than half in one place and about two-thirds in another). In tiny ablations (2B, single-digit tokenization) Beyond-K–5 pass@1 is 1.1%→1.8% vs Unfiltered 4.6%→10.3%. — "Curriculum exposure thus sets LittleLearner ’s capability frontier, and scale operates only within it." ## Limitations and conditions

- "we acknowledge that certain emergent behaviors like in-context learning may be less pronounced than at frontier scales" (6 Conclusion, line 230) — the negative ICL result may be a scale artifact.
- "In each case, it is the pretraining filter, rather than the intervention, that sets the effective capability ceiling in our tested settings." (4.4, line 204) — the headline is scoped to the tested settings.
- "Crucially, within the tested post-training budgets, we observe no difference" (4.2, line 180) — the post-training null is conditional on the (modest) budgets tried.
- "We do not find that for the 5B LittleLearner and the few shot and explanation setups we test, in-context learning (ICL) is able to unlock new reasoning capabilities." (4.3, line 194) — ICL null is for specific prompt setups at 5B.
- "This low K–5 retention reflects our deliberate precision-over-recall design" (3.1.6, line 102) — the corpus keeps about 35% of genuine K–5 material; the restriction is also a thinning of in-scope data.
- "The filter retains 2.48% of 6,000 Beyond-K–5 passages" (3.1.6, line 104) — some leakage on external data, though manual inspection finds few genuinely out-of-scope passages.
- "This trend partly reflects the limitations of grade-level CCSS labels as a proxy for problem difficulty for language models." (Mathematical Reasoning, line 156) — the grade axis is an imperfect difficulty measure.
- "LittleLearner ’s capabilities do not follow the ordering of a human curriculum." (Mathematical Reasoning, line 156) and "The developmental framing is therefore a tool for controlling exposure, not a claim of human-like development." (Educational science and human–model comparison, line 222) — not a model of a child.
- "without it, neither base model is fine-tunable into a coherent chat model" (C.4, line 480) — post-training depends on chat data folded into pretraining cooldown.
- "To test whether the Beyond-K–5 arithmetic gap is driven by number segmentation, we conduct a matched 2B ablation" (Appendix B, line 390) — the custom K–5 tokenizer is a possible confound; the ablation narrows the gap only partly.

## Other findings

- The draft omits the scaling experiment (0.6B/1.3B/5B), which is one of the three probes and part of the headline.
- The paper contradicts itself on Grade 8 at pass@1024. Section 3.3.2 says LittleLearner "solves fewer than half as many questions as Unfiltered"; Appendix C.2.3 says it retains `only about two-thirds` of Unfiltered's competence. Both quotes: "even at pass@1024, LittleLearner solves fewer than half as many questions as Unfiltered" (line 156); "but only about two-thirds of it at Grade 8 with no overlapping CIs" (line 460).
- The pretraining mix is not purely filtered web text: the cooldown includes 5% K–5-rewritten math reasoning, 2% math instruction and 2% general instruction data (Gemini rewrites).
- The draft's `result` carries a process note about `snippet_search`; it should be dropped now that the full text is read.
- Cluster placement (E. exploration, entropy collapse) is loose. The paper's relevance there is only the GRPO result that RL does not move capability beyond pretraining exposure.
