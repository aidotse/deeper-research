- `method summary`: corrected (completed) — Self-training arms are compared against a frozen copy of the base model pushed through the identical evaluation path, so that every statistic has a measured null. ## Limitations and conditions

- "what we establish is that this regime does not expand, not that self-training cannot" (6 Limitations, line 201) — three rounds and roughly 270 optimiser steps of rank-32 LoRA, far short of the schedules of the methods it positions against. The authors "cannot speak to accumulation" (E.3, line 925).
- "Expansion rests on $22$ low-base problems on one benchmark, and on the $10$ the base model never reaches the comparison is not significant." (6 Limitations, line 201) — limits the dissociation result to rarely-reached problems, not expansion.
- "it may in part be the perturbation of memorized answers rather than of reasoning" (E.3, line 925) — the corruption result rests on a band from MATH training problems that are likely in pretraining data.
- "its counts are therefore not a forgetting rate for a natural distribution" (Benchmarks, line 85) — the band is enriched near the decision boundary.
- "We therefore state cross-backbone generalization as untested rather than answer it with an underpowered arm." (E.3, line 925) — one backbone family carries every claim.
- "teacher capability and verbosity necessarily still differ, and F3 shows the latter is real" (E.3, line 925) — the distillation control is matched in stream, volume and evaluation, but not in teacher strength or style.
- "a method that trains only on what it already solves is a priori unlikely to expand into AIME-hard territory" (E.3, line 925) — the evolution stream is easy, and the null "does not speak to" curriculum-generating methods.
- "The additional seeds for the band arms were run on a $175$ -problem subsample rather than the full $1{,}163$" (E.3, line 925) — seed replication of the band is on a subsample.
- "under a pure endpoint rule the two self-training methods coincide exactly and at $z=0$ their order reverses, so we rest nothing on the between-method comparison" (5, Two things we can only bound, line 191) — STaR versus majority-vote ordering is not a supported result.
- "we report a bound (any change here is smaller than about eleven points) rather than a null result" (5, line 191) — the refusal/safety probe is inconclusive.
- "We report roughly a dozen $p$ -values across seven failure modes and correct within each family rather than across them" (6, A reporting standard, line 197) — multiple-comparison correction is within families only.
- "We do not claim that a majority-vote reward does this in general" (A.3, line 339) — the policy-gradient arm's collapse is claimed for this configuration only, and its transition counts are excluded.
- "that arm’s corruption counts are excluded from the capability analysis" (4.3, line 135) — distillation's corruption counts are excluded under F3 because of truncation and style drift.
- "every claim below is about what a stated sampling budget reaches, not about the support of the base distribution" (Expansion, line 77) — base-unreached means at k=128; zero successes bounds the base pass@1 at q ≤ 0.023, but does not show q = 0.
- "Two deviations from TTRL as published." (Benchmarks, line 89) — the majority-vote SFT arm is not TTRL. TTRL's on-set configuration is reported only as an unmatched baseline.

## Other findings

- On the draft's "inverts a reported finding": the paper's own wording is that each failure "inverts a reported finding when its control is absent" (Abstract, line 11). Table 1's "would have concluded" column shows that these are conclusions an uncontrolled analysis would reach in this study's own runs. They are not re-analyses of other papers' published claims.
- Of the 7 controls, "three of the seven cost nothing at all" (Table 1 caption, line 95). The F7 point is that four frozen replicates are not enough: "only at $G=9$ does it hold within $\pm 0.02$ of the value eleven give" (F7, line 143).
- The majority-vote outcome is seed-unstable. Accuracy changes are +4.0, −2.8 and −1.0 on the band, and +3.3, −1.5 and +0.7 on AIME: "a majority-vote method’s outcome is not determined by its design alone" (F5, line 139).
- Fit to block J: the paper concerns self-training loops and their measurement, and reports corruption alongside a mean-accuracy gain. It does not study recursive synthetic-data model collapse over many generations.
