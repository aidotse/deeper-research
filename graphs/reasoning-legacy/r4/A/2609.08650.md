## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — DATPO (Difficulty-Adaptive Sentence-entropy-guided Tree-structured Policy Optimization) — "we propose DATPO (Difficulty-Adaptive Sentence-entropy-guided Tree-structured Policy Optimization)" (Abstract, line 11)
- `method summary`: corrected — An RLVR method that changes the structure of train-time rollouts rather than the objective. It uses a two-phase tree rollout: N parallel base rollouts, then up to K forking points per trajectory chosen by sentence-level entropy, with B branch rollouts from each, and the branching budget is allocated by empirically estimated difficulty (more rollouts to hard problems). A block-level, Monte Carlo state-value advantage gets an annealed sibling-diversity bonus, computed from an external embedding model and applied only to blocks with positive base advantage. The design rests on a preliminary GRPO study in which larger rollout budgets lowered pass@256 when training on easy problems and raised it on hard ones, with a theoretical lens (Theorems A.1, A.2) built on an idealised assumption. — "the coefficient $\alpha$ is linearly annealed during training to gradually decay the exploration incentive" (3.2, line 152); "For models trained on the Easy dataset (1(a)), larger budgets degrade pass@256." (2.1.1, line 61); "A2 serves as an idealized first-order approximation to isolate the core mechanism" (Remark on Assumption 2, line 409)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — trained on MATH with Qwen2.5-3B-Base and Qwen3-4B-Base; evaluated on MATH500, AIME26, AIME25, AIME24 and AMC23 (out-of-domain check in the appendix on GPQA-Diamond and a 500-item stratified MMLU-Pro subset); baselines GRPO (clip-higher, token-level loss), Dr.GRPO, TreeRL, AttnRL — "We use Qwen2.5-3B-Base (Yang et al., 2024) and Qwen3-4B-Base (Yang et al., 2025) as base models. ## Limitations and conditions

- "calculating the sibling-diversity term requires additional forward passes through an external embedding model, which introduces computational overhead" (Limitations, line 303) — limits cost; the method depends on an external embedding model.
- "Deriving both the empirical difficulty and Monte Carlo state values from a limited number of rollouts (e.g., $N=4,B=4$ ) can inject noise into the estimations, occasionally yielding high variance during policy updates." (Limitations, line 305) — limits the reliability of the difficulty estimate and advantage.
- "resource limitations restricted our empirical validation to the 3B-4B parameter regime, leaving DATPO’s scalability to larger models ( $\geq 7$ B) unverified" (Limitations, line 307) — limits scope to 3B-4B models.
- "as our evaluation primarily focuses on mathematical reasoning, the framework’s direct effectiveness in other rigorous domains, such as complex logical reasoning or code generation, requires further investigation" (Limitations, line 307) — limits domain scope.
- "despite this token-level equivalence, tree-based methods generally incur a higher wall-clock time compared to standard parallel rollouts" (F.3, line 971) — the comparison is matched on generated tokens, not on wall-clock time; DATPO adds further embedding overhead.
- "To ensure a fair comparison, we maintain a comparable total number of generated tokens per problem across all methods while adopting the tree-expansion hyperparameters reported in the original TreeRL and AttnRL papers." (4.1, line 213) — condition on the headline comparison: baselines run at their published (6,2,2) topology while DATPO uses (4,3,4); Appendix F.6 checks this with a MATH500-only experiment.
- "A2 serves as an idealized first-order approximation to isolate the core mechanism" (Remark on Assumption 2, line 409) — assumption under Theorem A.1; the authors note the expected advantage magnitude "technically depends on $G$".
- "We do not explicitly model the variance reduction in advantage estimation to avoid complex distributional assumptions." (Remark on advantage-estimation variance, line 413) — limits what Theorem A.1 explains.
- "while these exact behavioral patterns may not identically manifest in every setup" (Connection to Empirical Regimes, line 525) — the easy/medium/hard rollout-budget regimes are setup-specific empirical observations, not a general law.
- "applying $k=8$ for MATH500 and $k=64$ for the remaining datasets" (4.3, line 233) — the test-time scaling result is on Qwen2.5-3B-Base only and at these k values.

## Other findings

- The paper does not test large-k pass@k against the base model (k is at most 64 in the main results, 256 in the Section 2.1 pilot). So it does not show that DATPO avoids the base-model crossover discussed in the cluster's create-versus-select debate; it shows higher pass@k than the base and RL baselines at k=8/64. The authors frame the motivation in that debate ("RLVR often fails to unlock new reasoning capabilities beyond the reasoning coverage of the base model", line 19).
- No mention of multiple training seeds was found (grep for "seed" found nothing); "three independent evaluation runs" refers to evaluation sampling only. UNVERIFIED whether training was run once, but no variance across training runs is reported, and several gaps are 1-3 points.
- The training-dynamics claim that difficulty labels were not given to the model ("these difficulty labels are used strictly for this analysis", line 227): DATPO's difficulty is estimated from rollouts, not from MATH level labels.
- Out-of-domain (Appendix F.4) gains are small and mixed: +2.4 GPQA-Diamond and -0.1 MMLU-Pro vs the strongest baseline on Qwen2.5-3B-Base.
- Ablations (forking strategy, sibling-diversity coefficient, wider topology) are reported on MATH500 only; the forking-strategy pass@8 differences are small (81.7 sent-entropy vs 81.6 random).
