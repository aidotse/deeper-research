## Limitations and conditions

- "These results are descriptive measurements in the single-call, prefilled-tool setting tested here." (5 Conclusions, line 155) — scope: one response, prefilled tool call.
- "Because the model does not select the prefilled tool call, this setting evaluates continuation after a tool result rather than tool-selection policy." (2.2, line 41) — tool-selection policy is not evaluated.
- "Our experiments show where verbalized commitment changes, but they do not identify why." (Limitations, line 159) — the mechanism is unidentified: training priors, reporting conventions or judge sensitivity.
- "models may use the trace as an optional report rather than as a necessary workspace" (Limitations, line 163) — the tasks may not require serial reasoning. "Our results may therefore differ for tasks where applying the cue requires substantial serial reasoning." (line 163)
- "their agreement does not rule out shared, channel-specific rubric error; the reported gaps are gaps under this rubric as applied by two automated judges" (Limitations, line 165) — the results depend on automated LLM judges.
- "We therefore emphasize the signs of within-model comparisons rather than differences in magnitude between families." (Limitations, line 165) — cross-family magnitudes are not to be compared.
- "The second judge reproduces our main findings, albeit absolute ( $\mathit{VCR}$ ) estimates and some model rankings depend on the judge." (2.4, line 63) — absolute VCR is judge-dependent: GPT-5.6-Luna gives higher VCR on every model.
- "The intercept is not a causal estimate of cue form." (3.2, line 103) — the explicitness gap may still reflect unmeasured clarity differences, and the models rate their own cues.
- "The four larger models are unrated, so cue reading above 284B is untested." (3.1, line 83) — the cue-readability check covers only 11 of the 15 models.
- "Model family, cue condition, or influence strength may affect both quantities, so this correlation is not causal." (3.3, line 117) — the UAR–AUROC correlation is descriptive; per-model fits use only four conditions.
- "Unlike the primary rates, the monitor evaluation uses all transcripts before the eval-awareness filter" (3.3, line 109) — the monitor and primary analyses use different transcript sets.
- "Each channel–explicitness condition reuses the same no-cue controls, so the four AUROC estimates are correlated." (3.3, line 113)
- "A narrowed gap is therefore not, by itself, a monitorability gain." (3.4, line 127) — the directive's narrowing often comes from rising user-channel UAR.
- "Several intervals lie near zero, so the exact count is sensitive to bootstrap resampling." (3.4, line 129) — the monitor-awareness counts are fragile.
- "Because parameter count is confounded with model family and post-training, this split cannot be attributed to scale." (3.2, line 101)
- "We cannot tell whether longer traces contain more substantive commitment, more formulaic attribution, or simply more text for the judge to score" (3.5, line 141) — the reasoning-effort effect may be a judge artefact.
- "Eval-awareness stays below 5% in every condition on 11 of 15 models, but is elevated on the user-channel elsewhere" (2.4, line 67) — eval-aware transcripts are excluded; up to 14.1% on Kimi K2.6 User (Explicit).

## Other findings

- The abstract says monitors have "worse ability to detect verbalization". The body's monitor task is to detect preference adoption (tailoring), scored against Claude Haiku 4.5 labels. The draft's wording should follow the body: "to read a transcript and score, on a $0$ – $100$ scale, how much the assistant tailored its answer to a discoverable preference" (3.3, line 111).
- With the single monitor-awareness disclosure tested, the authors did not find the CoT-monitorability erosion that Korbak et al. warn of: "With the single disclosure tested here, we do not find the erosion that Korbak et al. (2025) warn about." (3.4, line 129)
- OLMo's low tool-channel UAR comes from low cue following, not from faithful traces: "OLMo’s tool-channel conditions combine low cue following with low verbalized commitment, so unverbalized adoption remains low." (3.2, line 95)
- All 15 models are open-weight. No closed frontier reasoning model is evaluated as a subject.
