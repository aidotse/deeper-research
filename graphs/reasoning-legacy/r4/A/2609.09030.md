## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — answer-distribution trajectories — "We introduce answer-distribution trajectories, a stochastic-dynamics-inspired representation that tracks the model’s full predictive distribution over answers as reasoning unfolds." (Abstract, line 11)
- `method summary`: corrected (completed) — The draft is confirmed but omits how the trajectory is obtained and the classification that comes with it. At checkpoints every 16 CoT tokens, the predictive answer distribution is estimated by Monte Carlo: 16 independent continuations from the prefix, each parsed to a discrete answer label. Sampling uses T=0.7, top-p 0.9 and a maximum generation length of 600 tokens. Each trace is summarised by seven metrics in four groups:
  - exploration: effective support;
  - revision: dominance switches and returns;
  - motion: total-variation path length, its temporal concentration, and directness;
  - commitment: time to stable p ≥ τ.

  Traces are also classified into four success mechanisms and four failure mechanisms by gold-answer dominance. "Strictly finer" is a proved proposition: the endpoint and entropy maps are non-injective functions of the trajectory. — "In practice, we use $N=16$ independent continuations per prefix unless otherwise stated." (A.4, line 369); "spaced uniformly at stride $s=16$ , and always including the final prefix length of the trajectory" (A.5, line 377); "a maximum generation length of 600 tokens" (A.3, line 343); "Neither endpoint prediction nor entropy trajectory identifies the underlying answer-distribution dynamics." (3.2, Proposition 2, line 87)

## Limitations and conditions

- "the comparisons in Section 5.3 are only descriptive, and future controlled studies could better isolate the effects of architecture, scale, and post-training" (6 Conclusion, line 261) — the "training and inference choices systematically reshape" claim is correlational.
- "whether the information that answer-distribution trajectories provide can translate into practical gains remains an open question" (6 Conclusion, line 261) — no downstream utility is shown.
- "Our analysis is computationally expensive, developing cheaper approximations of answer-distribution trajectories is therefore an important direction" (6 Conclusion, line 261) — cost limits the method's applicability.
- "a maximum generation length of 600 tokens" (A.3, line 343) — CoTs are truncated at 600 tokens. The results concern short CoTs from mostly non-reasoning models, not long reasoning-model traces.
- "We focus on reasoning tasks with a discrete answer space" (A.1, line 271) — the method requires a parseable, discrete answer space.
- "In practice, we use $N=16$ independent continuations per prefix unless otherwise stated." (A.4, line 369) — each predictive distribution is a 16-sample estimate, and checkpoints are every 16 tokens. That bounds the resolution of switches and commitment.
- "Thus, $n=6$ for the instruction-tuning and model-scale panels and $n=4$ for the temperature panel." (Appendix B, Figure 4, line 425) — the intervention effects rest on 6 or 4 matched comparisons. The temperature comparison uses GSM8K only, with 4 models.
- "Commitment is undefined if no hypothesis remains above the threshold through the end of the trajectory." (4.1, line 133) — t_commit is missing for non-committing traces.
- "An important observation is that the same dynamical pattern can produce different outcomes depending on which hypotheses the trajectory explores and ultimately favors." (4.2, line 183) — the profiles do not determine the outcome.
- "Increasing model scale shows a weaker, less uniform tendency toward shifts in the same direction" (5.3, line 251) — the scale effect is weak.

## Other findings

- The "four reasoning benchmarks" are easy, older sets: GSM8K, ARC, SVAMP and MATH algebra. Only one model, DeepSeek-R1-distilled-7B, is labelled as an RL-trained reasoning model. The row's placement in a reasoning-model theory block should be read in that light.
- The paper labels DeepSeek-R1-distilled-7B "reasoning-specialized RL model" (A.2, line 317). From outside knowledge, the R1-distilled models are SFT-distilled rather than RL-trained; this was not checked against retrieved text: UNVERIFIED.
- The paper's "stochastic-dynamics" framing is a representation choice. There is no dynamical model fitted or derived; the theory consists of Propositions 1–2 and Corollary 1 (Appendix C).
- Section 4.2 says the prevalence of mechanisms is quantified "in Section 3.2" (line 139). The prevalence actually appears in Section 5.1 / Figure 2. This is an internal cross-reference slip.
