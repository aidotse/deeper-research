## Cells

- `method name`: corrected (minor) — the paper names no method. It calls its contribution a "high-dimensional analytical framework", so the draft's label is a fair descriptive label but not the paper's name. — "we develop a high-dimensional analytical framework to study class-dependent learning in score-based diffusion models" (Abstract, line 11)
- `method summary`: corrected — The draft is right but omits the assumptions the result rests on. The score is a random-features model: tanh features with a fixed Gaussian projection and trainable readout, trained by gradient flow from zero initialisation. Its dynamics are exactly solvable as a linear ODE. The data are an isotropic Gaussian mixture. The analysis is in the high-dimensional proportional limit (N, P, M to infinity at fixed ratios). It uses conditional Gaussian equivalence and the replica method under a replica-symmetric ansatz. The timescale results are restricted to binary mixtures at small diffusion time (t << 1). — "we analyze these training regimes using Random Matrix Theory to characterize the spectrum of $\mathbf{U}$ in the high-dimensional proportional limit" (2 Problem Formulation, line 88); "For clarity, we restrict our focus to a binary Gaussian mixture ( $C=2$ ) evaluated at small diffusion times ( $t\ll 1$ )." (3.2, line 148); "We impose a Replica-Symmetric (RS) ansatz" (F.1, line 793)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — Theory: an RF score model on a Gaussian mixture, checked against finite-size gradient-descent simulations (N=100). Validation: DDPM with a U-Net backbone on binary Fashion-MNIST subsets (Sneaker paired with each of the 9 other classes, at Sneaker proportions 25/50/75%, 8,000 images). Further validation: CIFAR (one class pair, 3,000 samples, appendix). Plus an RF model on a synthetic GMM parametrised by the Fashion-MNIST class moments. ResNet-18 classifiers assign generated samples to classes. — "Each pair is trained at three Sneaker proportions (25%, 50%, 75%) on 8,000 images." (4, Model, Training, and Generation, line 227); "We further present the same phenomenon on CIFAR [29] using the same DDPM architecture" (J.2, line 1399)
- `metric`: corrected (refined) — For the theory: class-specific speciation times, the optimal generalisation time τ_g^(c) (argmin of the class-conditional test error), and the memorisation onset τ_m^(c) (where the test error returns to 1). For DDPM: per-class memorisation fraction from a nearest-neighbour distance ratio below 1/3, the threshold-crossing time at f_mem ≥ 1/3, and the Sneaker-partner memorisation gap. — "We define the threshold-crossing time $t_{\mathrm{mem}}^{c}=\inf\{t:f_{\mathrm{mem}}^{c}(t)\geq 1/3\}$ , yielding the Sneaker-partner memorization gap" (4 Evaluation, line 231)
- `result`: corrected (in part) — The theory content is confirmed. Variance is the dominant factor, with higher-variance classes generalised and memorised earlier. Smaller-norm centroids come second. Imbalance is "usually a subleading effect" that can invert the order. Strong imbalance, with b_c = O(N^{-a_c}), delays minority-class speciation to t = (1-a_c)/2 log N. The draft omits a stated trade-off: closing the generalisation gap by reweighting widens the memorisation gap. The DDPM validation is qualitative only, and the RF and DDPM gaps sit on different scales. The draft's note that snippet search returned only the abstract is obsolete. Its note that the paper is a single model trained once, not a self-training loop, is confirmed: no recursive or synthetic-data training appears in the body. — "Sampling Imbalance: While usually a subleading effect, strong imbalance acts as a modulator capable of inverting the learning order established by variance and geometry." (3.2.1, Result 2, line 197); "adjusting sampling weights to close the generalization gap between classes inevitably widens the memorization gap, and vice versa." (Result 3, line 221); "Despite operating on distinct gap scales, the models share similar descriptor-dependent patterns" (Result 4, line 245)

## Limitations and conditions

- "our presentation focused on binary Gaussian mixtures with isotropic covariances" (5 Conclusions, line 261) — limits the scope of the derived results: C=2 and isotropic covariances.
- "it is fundamentally restricted to capturing first- and second-order structures" (5 Conclusions, line 261) — the theory cannot capture outliers or multimodal within-class structure, and the authors leave the effect of "more complex heterogeneities" open.
- "extending the theory to more realistic class structure and conditional settings would be an important next step" (5 Conclusions, line 261) — conditional sampling and guidance are out of scope.
- "We leave a systematic study of such interventions to future work." (5 Conclusions, line 259) — reweighting or rebalancing as a mitigation is suggested, not tested.
- "Unless stated otherwise, we assume $\forall\ c,\ ||\bm{m}_{c}||=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$ and $\sigma_{c}=\mathcal{O}(1)$ ." (Data Distribution, line 70) — scaling assumptions on the centroids and variances.
- "Assuming an initialization of $\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}$ , the exact solution is given by" (Training Dynamics, line 74) — zero initialisation and gradient flow on a linear-in-parameters RF model.
- "it is unlikely to be observed empirically: finite learning rates effectively bypass the associated small-eigenvalue directions" (3.2, line 152) — the final memorisation timescale τ_mem^(2) is theoretical only.
- "While first and second-order moments do not fully resolve the complexity of memorization—as evidenced by residual discrepancies" (4 Results and Discussion, line 255) — the agreement between RF and DDPM is qualitative, with residual mismatch.
- "Because memorization has no unique operational definition, choosing comparable thresholds across models is inherently difficult." (J.2 Robustness, line 1407) — the empirical memorisation times depend on a threshold choice. The authors report that the trends hold at 1/6 and 1/12.
- "To isolate centroid effects, we exclude relatively extreme variance cases" (Figure 5 caption, line 235) — the centroid-geometry confirmation on DDPM uses a filtered subset.
- "Strong Imbalance: When a class is severely undersampled ( $b_{c}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-a_{c}})$ for $0<a_{c}<1$ ), its transition is delayed." (Result 1, line 128) — the delayed-speciation result requires imbalance that scales with dimension. Under O(1) imbalance, all classes speciate together.

## Other findings

- Result 1 is derived for exact models. The intro words the strong-imbalance effect as minority classes becoming "inferable at distinct earlier diffusion times". In backward-process terms, that is the delay the abstract describes, so the two framings are consistent. A reader should still know that "earlier diffusion time" means later in generation.
- An equivalent delay of speciation also arises without imbalance, when a class centroid is subextensive: "an equivalent hierarchy of speciation times emerges even under balanced and weak imbalanced sampling" (3.1, line 136).
- Structural class diversity contracts the generalisation window and expands the memorisation window, relative to a unimodal baseline. The draft does not mention this: "strong class diversity (i.e. $v$ deviating significantly from $1$ ) reliably contracts the generalization window" (3.2, line 154).
- The fit to block J (self-training and model collapse) is weak, as the draft itself notes. The paper contains no recursive or synthetic-data training.
