The label should be marked as descriptive, e.g. `obfuscation generalisation (leave-one-out reward-hacking RL with CoT or summary penalties)`. — "We show generalisation by using a leave-one-out round-robin over four (modified) reward hacking datasets" (1 Introduction, Figure 1 caption, line 14)
- `method summary`: corrected — The authors use GRPO to train Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B on three of four modified reward-hacking MCQ datasets. Reward hacking is deliberately rewarded. A small LLM-overseer penalty, Kimi-K2 or Kimi-K2.5, is added in one of two places: on the CoT (CoT pressure), or on a summary between `</think>` and `<answer>` (output supervision, CoT never penalised). Evaluation is on the held-out fourth dataset and on a new high-stakes medical-sycophancy set that is never trained on. The measures are reward-hacking rate and CoT and summary monitor flag rates, using the same monitor prompts as in training. The summary monitor is deliberately stricter than the CoT monitor. A no-penalty control is included. — "We train models using GRPO (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025) on a composite reward" (3 Methods, Model training, line 80); "penalty for revealing reward hacking behaviour, applied to either CoT pressure or outcome supervision via the summary" (line 80); "For all datasets, summary penalisation is stricter; simply mentions of the additional information is flagged" (line 84). ## Limitations and conditions

- "we only study this phenomenon on a single model family and two model sizes" (5.1 Limitations, line 140) — limits generality across families, sizes and training pipelines.
- "our results should not be taken as representative example of how obfuscation could occur in a production RL environment" (5.1, line 142) — the settings are constructed, simple and unrealistic: obviously leaked scores and stated preferences.
- "Further work is necessary to understand how obfuscation generalises when the undesired behaviour typically requires CoT to be performed." (5.1, line 144) — the tasks are non-load-bearing; obfuscation is known to be rarer where the CoT is necessary (Emmons et al., 2025).
- "The examples below are drawn from CoT-penalisation runs; equivalent OOF behaviour under summary penalisation is left to future work." (Appendix I, line 455) — the abstract's "formats unseen during training" claim is not tested for the output-supervision condition.
- "Across four runs, we observed obfuscation transfer in three" (Appendix I, line 455) — the out-of-format result rests on 4 runs, and 1 failed to transfer.
- "the absolute detection rate may be a noisy signal in either direction" (I.1, line 469) — the CoT monitor gives false positives on degenerate CoTs, so detection-rate drops are partly confounded by trace degeneration.
- "We note two examples where the model learns to obfuscate reasoning about reward hacking without any penalisation" (C.1, line 196) — obfuscation-like behaviour also appears in 2 no-penalty control runs, through vacuous reasoning. This weakens the attribution of obfuscation to the penalty in some runs.
- "Few exceptions to generalisation do exist, typically for the code selection and revealed score MMLU tasks." (Appendix C, Figure 10 caption, line 190) — reward-hacking generalisation is not universal.
- "These are considered ‘failed runs’, arising randomly due to training instabilities." (Appendix B, Figure 9 caption, line 186) — failed runs are excluded from aggregation, and checkpoints with more than 30% parsing failures are excluded: "We exclude data points with parsing errors $>$ 30% in our core analysis." (Appendix D, Figure 13 caption, line 220).
- "The decision to use a more strict summary penalisation than CoT penalisation is motivated by modern training practices" (Model training, line 84) — the output-supervision result depends on a strict summary monitor that flags any mention of the leaked information.
- "A significant portion of our experimental iterations involved refining the monitors’ system prompt." (H.9, line 425) — the monitor prompts and datasets were iterated, including rephrasing questions to avoid overseer failures (H.10.3).
- "The overseer penalty is set at $-0.05$ , and is only applied when the model’s answer is correct" (H.6, line 353) — the penalty is small and conditional on reward-hacking success. That is an assumption of the setup.

## Other findings

- The draft's dataset cell omits the policy models (Qwen3-4B/8B), the monitor models and the medical-sycophancy hold-out. These are the evaluation setup.
- On the CoT-penalisation condition, the draft's method summary says obfuscation is "explicitly trained by the authors". That is accurate. Note also that the authors' summary monitor is stricter by design, so the output-supervision condition is not a neutral analogue of RLHF.
- The authors read their result as evidence for both of Drori et al.'s spillover mechanisms. That is their interpretation and is not separately tested: "Our findings indicate that both mechanisms can drive CoT obfuscation across task domains" (5 Discussion, line 136).
- The authors' conclusion: "The standard recommendation to avoid applying optimisation pressure directly to the CoT is therefore necessary but not sufficient." (6 Conclusion, line 150)
