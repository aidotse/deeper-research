## Cells

- `method name`: corrected (minor) — access-versus-execution decomposition over Countdown entrance families. "Entrance family" is the paper's term; "entrance-family localisation" is not. — "Access vs. Execution Decomposition: We introduce an exhaustive state-space decomposition framework on Countdown to separate solution initiation from downstream reasoning execution." (1 Introduction, line 37)
- `method summary`: corrected (measurement and conditions added) — An exact solver enumerates every valid Countdown solution. Solutions are grouped into entrance families by their first operand and operator. Solving via a family factorises into access (does the policy pick the family) and execution (does it complete validly once there). Access is measured by free sampling and by teacher-forced prefix likelihoods. Execution is measured interventionally, by clamping a solver-built entrance prefix. On math benchmarks without enumerable solution spaces, the first complete calculation serves as an observable proxy for an entrance, scored with first-calculation entropy, distinct-trace rate and same-trace likelihood split. — "Because an entrance family fixes only the initial operation while leaving all subsequent arithmetic unconstrained, solving a problem through family $b$ factorizes into an access phase and an execution phase" (3, line 71); "On standard math benchmarks the first complete calculation is an observable proxy for an entrance, not a solver-defined one." (Appendix A, line 249)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — Countdown in two setups. (1) A self-trained PPO run on Qwen2.5-3B (TinyZero; 150 held-out problems, 320 rollouts, 256-token cap). (2) The public Qwen-2.5-3B-R1-countdown GRPO series on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, which the authors evaluate but did not train (135 overlap-filtered problems). Math suite: GSM8K, MATH500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, AMC23, AIME24, with 64 samples each. Models there: Qwen2.5-7B/14B Base vs SimpleRL checkpoints, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, and the OLMo-3 7B SFT/DPO/RLVR series. SFT controls are LoRA on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct. — "we train Qwen2.5-3B (Qwen et al., 2025) with PPO, evaluating the base model alongside eleven actor checkpoints saved from step 25 to step 275." (3, line 61); "we evaluate Qwen2.5-7B/14B Base and SimpleRL checkpoints (Zeng et al., 2025), DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (Guo et al., 2025), and the full OLMo-3 7B alignment progression (SFT, DPO, and RLVR)" (3, line 83)
- `metric`: corrected (additions) — On Countdown: exhaustive solution coverage (the fraction of canonical valid solutions reached by n rollouts), pass@1, per-token NLL shift at the entrance versus during execution, and designated-family completion under a clamped entrance. On math benchmarks: pass@k, first-calculation entropy (nats) and distinct-trace rate. — "its categorical entropy serves as our primary metric for early-stage search breadth." (3, line 81)
- `result`: corrected (numbers confirmed; conditions added) —
  - Coverage falls by up to 67%: from 0.337 to 0.111 under PPO, while pass@1 rises more than fiftyfold. Under GRPO coverage falls 43% while accuracy triples.
  - Per-token NLL shift at the entrance versus during execution is +4.50 vs +0.28 (PPO, about 16x) and +8.08 vs +0.71 (GRPO, about 11x).
  - A minimal entrance prefix lifts designated-family completion from 0.018 to 0.212 (PPO step 275); under GRPO the lift is 0.104 to 0.188.
  - Layer interpolation (late layers 20–28 blended with step-50 weights) lifts coverage from 0.111 to 0.152 (+37%). Its pass@1 is 0.305, but the Δpass@1 interval of [-0.009, 0.065] spans zero, and the row is not marked equivalent under the paper's TOST test. Surface prompting and forced operators fail; higher temperature costs pass@1.
  - SFT reaches coverage 0.709 against 0.268 for the late GRPO checkpoint (2.6x), but at lower pass@1 (0.333 vs 0.429).
  - At 7B and 14B, SimpleRL raises pass@1 while pass@64 stays flat, and first-calculation entropy falls 30% and 41%. The OLMo-3 staged pipeline keeps entropy.

  — "under PPO, pass@1 increases more than fiftyfold while overall solution coverage plummets from $0.337$ to $0.111$ ." ## Limitations and conditions

- "Exhaustive enumeration is what makes access and execution separately measurable, which currently ties the direct analysis to Countdown." (Appendix A, line 249) — the clean access/execution decomposition exists only on Countdown.
- "Our PPO run uses rollout group size $G{=}1$ , no actor KL penalty, and a 1,024-token training rollout cap" (Appendix A, line 249) — the self-trained PPO configuration is non-standard (G=1, no KL), which may exaggerate narrowing.
- "the second run is a public GRPO series we evaluate rather than train; and SFT controls use LoRA fine-tuning." (Appendix A, line 249) — the authors do not control the GRPO run; the SFT comparison uses LoRA.
- "On standard math benchmarks the first complete calculation is an observable proxy for an entrance, not a solver-defined one." (Appendix A, line 249) — the generalisation to math benchmarks rests on a proxy.
- "The supplied-entrance quantity $E_{t}^{\mathrm{do}}$ is interventional: it measures completion after an entrance is set externally, whereas the execution term in Equation 2 conditions on entrances selected by the policy itself." (Appendix A, line 249) — the claim that "latent execution capability remains intact" (8 Conclusion, line 245) is about forced entrances, not the policy's own.
- "In Countdown, selecting an entrance leaves only 6–10 tokens of downstream computation, making entrance access near-exclusive determinant of success." (7, line 207) — the strength of the entrance localisation is partly a property of the short-horizon task.
- "On complex, multi-step tasks, entrance selection remains primary gatekeeper, but long-horizon execution acts as a compounding secondary bottleneck." (7, line 207) — a second bottleneck on longer tasks.
- "the late-policy completion advantage decreases as more downstream computation remains." (F.4, line 781) — execution weakens with horizon.
- "although with single-sample accuracy shifting toward the early checkpoint." (6, line 193) — the largest coverage gain (checkpoint sampling, +0.100) costs pass@1 (0.161 vs 0.278).
- "tuning hyperparameter configurations on a 50-problem calibration set and reporting paired evaluation results on the remaining 100 held-out problems" (6, line 189) — the intervention results come from 100 problems.
- "At step 50, this structured allocation yields no significant advantage over free sampling" (6, line 195) — entrance allocation helps only after narrowing.

## Other findings

- The interpolation gain is stated as 37% in the abstract, introduction and Section 6, but the conclusion (line 245) says "restore up to 38% of solution coverage at invariant pass@1". This is a minor internal inconsistency.
- The abstract's "at no loss in pass@1" is a point-estimate statement. The Δpass@1 interval [-0.009, 0.065] includes zero. Unlike three other rows in Table 4, the interpolation row carries no TOST-equivalence mark.
- The SFT comparison is not at matched accuracy: SFT pass@1 is 0.333 against 0.429 for the GRPO checkpoint. "preserves more than double the coverage" (Abstract, line 11) should be read with that in mind.
- The 11x–16x figure is per-token NLL increase at the entrance versus during execution, measured between early and late checkpoints.
- The draft's note that body text was unavailable is obsolete.
- The paper is arXiv v1; code is released.
