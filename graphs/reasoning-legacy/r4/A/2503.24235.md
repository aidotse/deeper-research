## Limitations and conditions

- "researchers typically choose a specific “what to scale” based on an empirical hypothesis, aiming to achieve performance gains." (2 What to Scale, line 47) — the taxonomy's first axis classifies methods by the hypothesis their authors adopted, not by a validated mechanism.
- "We categorize various techniques, recognizing that a single work may involve multiple techniques" (1 Introduction, line 23) — classification is many-to-many; a work's place in the taxonomy is not unique.
- "parallel scaling remains has diminishing returns when coverage reaches saturation." (8.1 Parallel Scaling, line 1072) — authors' stated limit on the gains of parallel scaling.
- "Current test-time scaling methods are often highly specialized, limiting their generalizability." (8.1 Hybrid Scaling, line 1098) — limits how far surveyed results generalise.
- "While current hybrid scaling is mostly studied in controlled benchmarks, future work must consider its role in real-world applications." (8.1 Hybrid Scaling, line 1106) — evidence base for hybrid scaling is benchmark-bound.
- "Are there fundamental constraints on how much test-time scaling can improve reasoning performance?" (8.2, line 1142) — the survey flags the scaling question as open; it does not answer it.
- "As empirical observations on certain property models (xAI, 2025) show blindly scaling over test-time may lead to over-thinking." (8.2, line 1150) — a stated caveat on scaling test-time compute.
- "We plan to continuously update our taxonomy to reflect ongoing progress" (1 Introduction, line 39) — the taxonomy is a moving target; the version read is arXiv v3.

## Other findings

- The survey makes no quantitative claim of its own; its "trends" (Sec. 6) are a classification table of representative works, not a measured field statistic. A reader should not cite it for how well TTS scales.
- Section 8.2 raises open questions directly relevant to cluster A (whether PRMs help, optimal stopping, fundamental limits on TTS gains); these are questions, not findings.
