## Limitations and conditions

- "The models described in this paper are ultimately still a proof-of-concept." (10 Conclusions, line 458) — single model, single run; scope of all results.
- "our main checkpoint is trained for only 47000 steps on a broadly untested mixture, and the learning rate is never cooled down from its peak" (5.1 Standard Benchmarks, line 260) — caveats on the absolute benchmark numbers.
- "due to compute and time constraints, we were not able to ablate this mixture" (Pretraining Data., line 170) — the data mix (skewed to code/math, with instruction data mixed in) is a confound for the reasoning gains vs other open models.
- "Following Allen-Zhu and Li (2024), we directly mix relevant instruction data into the pretraining data." (Pretraining Data., line 170) — qualifies the `no specialised training data` framing.
- "the 800B token count, while large in comparison to older fully open-source models such as the Pythia series, is small in comparison to modern open-source efforts such as OLMo" (5.1, line 260) — comparison conditions.
- "it is not straightforward to place our model in direct comparison to other large language models" (5.1, line 260) — comparability caveat; the model consumes pretraining FLOPs close to a 32B transformer.
- "it chews through raw FLOPs close to what a 32B parameter transformer would consume during pretraining" (5.1, line 260) — the 3.5B parameter count understates compute; a matched-compute reading changes the comparison.
- "We find that saturation is highly task-dependent, on easier tasks the model saturates quicker" (5.3, line 348) — the test-time scaling gain is task-dependent and saturates.
- "the recurrent models has less capacity to memorize facts but more capacity to reason about its context" (Recurrence and Context, line 378) — fewer facts memorized; closed-book knowledge tasks lag.
- "At small scales all normalization strategies worked, and we observed only tiny differences between initializations. The same was not true at scale." (4.3, line 244) — two failed runs (representation collapse; a run that ignored the recurrent state); the recipe is fragile to norm/initialization/learning-rate choices.
- "there is concern that this comes with costs in model oversight in comparison to verbalized chains of thought" (Potential Implications of This Work, line 494) — oversight cost of latent reasoning.
- "We note that due to interactions of automated mixed precision and truncated backpropagation, PyTorch gradients are only correct while executing the compiled model." (A.2 Device Speed Details, line 504) — implementation condition.

## Other findings

- The paper does not compare against an explicit-CoT model at matched compute. The draft's open question resolves to: the controlled baseline is a fixed-depth twin (same architecture, one core pass) trained to 180B tokens only, not to 800B; GSM8K `CoT` numbers are the model itself prompted with 8-shot CoT, so the headline GSM8K gains combine verbalized CoT prompting with latent recurrence.
- The best GSM8K figure comes from an EMA weight-averaged model at r=64 (47.23% flexible, 38.59% strict), a separate artifact from the main checkpoint.
- The draft's `method summary` carries process notes about `snippet_search`; these should be dropped now that the full text is read.
- Additional contributions not in the draft: zero-shot per-token adaptive compute, KV-cache sharing, continuous CoT (warm-starting the state), and self-speculative decoding (Section 6), plus latent-trajectory analyses (`orbiting`) in Section 7.
