- `method summary`: corrected — Defines monitorability as a two-sided property (model informativeness and monitor capacity; faithfulness explicitly not required) and builds a 13-evaluation suite across three archetypes: intervention (a controlled change to input; can the monitor tell why behaviour changed), process (problems with a small known set of admissible solution paths), and outcome-property (an unambiguously labelled property of the output, e.g. passing tests on an impossible task). Monitors are given one of three scopes (CoT-only, action-only, all-messages); GPT-5 Thinking at high effort is the default monitor. The suite is applied to frontier models, to two frontier RL runs, to a pretraining-scale series, to agent-versus-monitor compute, to RL runs deliberately rewarded for CoT obfuscation, and to follow-up questioning. ## Limitations and conditions

- "we underscore that these results only represent two experiments and more thorough study is needed." (6.1, line 327) — the headline "RL does not decrease monitorability" rests on two training runs.
- "While our results suggest that reinforcement learning does not meaningfully degrade CoT monitorability at the current frontier scale, it still very well could at increased scale." (9, line 385) — scope of the RL result is current scale only.
- "in principle one should expect RL to teach a model to compress its CoT and, in the presence of length penalties, suppress any externalized information" (6.1, line 329) — the authors' own expectation runs against extrapolating the RL result.
- "suggesting our metric does not properly account for problems that are noise-dominated and that we likely need to revise our thresholding." (6.1, line 327) — a stated weakness of the metric (seen on sandbagging during o3 RL).
- "One limitation of these results is that we only tested monitorability with OpenAI models serving as monitors." (4, line 301) — OpenAI-model results may be inflated by shared idiosyncrasies between model and monitor; this bears on the cross-vendor comparison.
- "For this plot, we only ran the subset of our evals that do not require tools." (Figure 1 caption, line 16) — external-model comparisons exclude tool-using environments.
- "This excludes models such as the Gemini-2.5/3 series and Claude 4.x series which only share summarized reasoning in their API." (4, line 293) — model coverage limit.
- "A major limitation of these experiments is that we only trained with a single seed at each model scale." (6.2, line 341) — the monitorability-tax result; the medium model is "very off trend".
- "our results only consider capability evaluations that are somewhat in-distribution for these models" (6.2, line 341) — the tax trade-off may differ out of distribution; latency costs were not considered.
- "Our experiments are relatively limited as we did not do broad sweeps or ablations, so we do not make any strong claims." (5, line 311) — the obfuscation-detection result.
- "This behavior seems unlikely to be steganography, though it is hard to fully rule that out." (5, line 309) — caveat on the generic obfuscation organism.
- "Our evaluations do not disentangle the propensity to verbalize versus the necessity to verbalize" (9, line 381) — what low monitorability (e.g. on sycophancy) means is unresolved.
- "because we only fix the environments that we can catch, this constitutes a slow evolutionary pressure for CoTs to obfuscate." (9, line 383) — the "no CoT pressure" condition of the RL result is porous; deliberative alignment is also noted as CoT optimisation.
- "datapoints for similar models and reasoning efforts may be incomparable when comparing two different plots." (Appendix B, line 516) — evaluations with too few positives are dropped per figure, so aggregate numbers are not comparable across figures.
- "All of our evaluations require that the model first do a specific behavior before we can ask if a monitor can detect that behavior." (2, line 74) — monitorability is only measurable where the target behaviour occurs.
- "In this setting, follow-up questions can actively degrade monitorability." (7, line 359) — limit on the follow-up-question result (e.g. Scruples).
- "A key limitation of our current degeneracy benchmark is that many of the negative examples are manually constructed" (E.4, line 967) — limit on the monitor-degeneracy evaluation.

## Other findings

- The draft's `result` presents "monitorability survives this optimisation pressure in the regimes tested" without the authors' two-run caveat or their explicit warning that it could fail at larger scale; both should travel with the claim.
- The obfuscation "model organisms" are described by the authors as "simple"; the generic one lost monitorability mainly by shortening its CoT, and the targeted one barely generalised (1 of 13 evals). "Catches model organisms" should be read in that light.
- Monitorability "tends to increase with the agents’ CoT length" (Figure 1 caption, line 16), which is a correlation; the RL-run trends "largely correlate with the amount of thinking tokens".
- Author affiliation matters for reading the vendor comparison: OpenAI models scored higher with OpenAI monitors, a confound the authors name.
