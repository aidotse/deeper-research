## Limitations and conditions

- "We primarily evaluate compound tasks using final-output accuracy." (Limitations and future work, line 230) — limits what is measured: intermediate-state correctness is not scored.
- "our preliminary studies found that requiring intermediate outputs reduced final accuracy, and potentially constrained models’ ability to reason freely" (Limitations and future work, line 230) — an evaluation design choice that shapes the results; JSON Structured Output was dropped for the same reason.
- "our empirical evaluation focuses on controlled data-structure tasks and broader validation on real-world benchmarks remains an important direction" (Limitations and future work, line 230) — limits external validity; the real-world evidence is the BFCL pilot only.
- "showing preliminary evidence that the decomposed-to-composed asymmetry can extend to practical settings" (Abstract, line 10) — the tool-calling result is explicitly preliminary (one model, 300 test examples).
- "Models are trained on small problem lengths (5-10) and evaluated on larger ones (up to 30)." (Figure 3 caption, line 112) — condition on the headline result: much of the compound-task gap is measured under length extrapolation.
- "averaged across three runs with the standard deviation shown as a shaded region" (Figure 3 caption, line 112) — the main Qwen3-4B results rest on three runs.
- "We train for 30 optimization steps with learning rate $5\times 10^{-6}$ ." (C.5, line 730) — condition: RL training is very short (30 steps, 5 rollouts per prompt); the asymmetry is shown for this budget only.
- "We note RL-Compound also degrades sharply as problem length increases" (4.4, line 164) — limits the positive side of the result at Level III.
- "composed-task training remains robust, though its gains depend on structural difficulty" (4.5, line 168) — conditions the distribution-shift claim.
- "This assumption removes possible additional errors caused by embedding an invocation in a compound prompt, so the resulting transfer guarantee is optimistic." (Context Invariance, line 310) — assumption under the theory; the authors call the bound optimistic.
- "Note that the coverage assumption assumes atomic supervision is capable of producing the contexts a compound rollout visits. This holds for path-structured graphs" (Failure and mistake, line 426) — the Lemma 5.1 coverage assumption holds for chains but not in general (not for branch-merge graphs).
- "Therefore, under a non-recoverability assumption, good compound performance directly constrains the model’s error on the first-step context" (5.2, line 208) — the compound-to-atomic explanation assumes a unique source node and that a first-step mistake cannot be recovered from.
- "Thus, even perfect atomic accuracy cannot rule out compound failure without additional assumptions linking performance on singleton and multi-invocation contexts." (5.1, line 204) — scope of the theory at Level III.

## Other findings

- Model coverage is uneven. The cross-family check (OLMo-3-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) covers only multi-skill chains; Levels I and III and the BFCL pilot use Qwen3-4B-Instruct alone.
- The authors deliberately used instruct models, excluding models "already heavily post-trained for reasoning" (4.1, line 124). So the result is about RL post-training of instruct models, not reasoning models.
- The draft's `result` cell carries a process note ("`snippet_search` returned no results ... claims remain sourced from the abstract only"). That note is not a property of the paper and no longer applies once the full text is read.
- The paper's practical conclusion goes beyond the measured contrast: "RL post-training cannot rely on decomposed skill supervision alone" (7, line 226). This is supported only within the controlled tasks and the pilot.
- The SFT-vs-RL comparison (Figure 6, multi-skill chains) is a secondary finding that the draft omits. It is relevant to the cluster: RL length-generalises better than SFT on programmatic CoT traces.
