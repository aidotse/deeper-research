## Cells

- `method name`: confirmed — Monotone Frontier Curriculum (MFC) — "we further identified a distribution-shift cost inherent to guided curricula and proposed Monotone Frontier Curriculum (MFC), whose per-sample frontier descends monotonically under a success-only update" (7 Conclusion)
- `method summary`: corrected — The draft describes the general family; this is MFC itself.
  - The family: teacher-guided backward chaining, in which a prefix of a teacher trace is prepended as a hint and the hint is gradually withdrawn. This is shared with the Mixture (R3-style) and AdaBack baselines.
  - MFC's own contribution is the scheduling rule. Each problem keeps one guidance frontier g_curr. Each visit draws the hint length uniformly from {0, …, g_curr}, and the frontier is lowered only after a success (mean reward ≥ τ = 0.5); failures leave the state unchanged ("success commits, failure is forgotten").
  - The motivation is a "distribution-shift cost". Guided training prompts differ from the unguided test prompts, and AdaBack's unguided training mass M0 plateaus at about 0.40.
  - All variants run on a shared GRPO core with DAPO stabilisers.

  — "It restructures AdaBack’s update around an asymmetric principle: success commits, failure is forgotten." (3.3, line 135); "Whenever $g(s,\rho)>0$ , the curriculum induces a distribution shift between training and test prompts." (3.2, line 121)

## Limitations and conditions

- "Our work relies on a single fixed teacher model (DeepSeek-V3.2) to generate all reasoning traces, and the quality of the resulting curriculum is inevitably tied to the quality of these traces." (Limitations, line 289) — the result is conditional on one strong teacher.
- "We do not study how teacher capability interacts with curriculum design." (Limitations, line 289) — teacher strength is untested.
- "The framework is trained only on mathematical reasoning with single-turn LLM outputs." (Limitations, line 291) — domain scope.
- "Transfer to code generation Li et al. (2022) or formal theorem proving is untested." (Limitations, line 291) — agentic tasks are also untested.
- "all under matched compute and matched $1{,}200$ GRPO steps" (4.4, line 237) — the 16× is data efficiency in source problems. The 128 problems also come with teacher traces from a much stronger model, which the GRPO baseline does not get.
- "Mixture is competitive on Qwen3-1.7B but trails GRPO on the Qwen3-0.6B average" (4.2, line 217) — "128 unsolvable problems match or exceed 2,000" holds for AdaBack and MFC, not for every curriculum.
- "On Qwen3-0.6B, the corresponding gaps range from $0.7$ to $3.3$ pp." (4.2, line 217) — on in-distribution OR1-200 and MATH-500, the 128-problem methods trail GRPO slightly. "Match" means within these gaps.
- "we evaluate Qwen3-1.7B at $\mathrm{pass}@64$ and retain the 147 problems with $\mathrm{pass}@64=0$" (4.1, line 199) — "unsolvable" is defined at pass@64 for the 1.7B model only. Uns-128 is reused unchanged for 0.6B.

## Other findings

- The SFT control isolates the training objective. SFT on the same 128 full teacher traces reaches 39.8, against 47.3 for MFC and 36.8 for Base. The authors argue the gain is "not attributable to teacher-trace distillation" (5.2, line 265).
- The two base models are both small Qwen3 models from one family. I found no mention of seeds, variance or confidence intervals for the main results (searched "seed", "variance", "confidence interval", "standard deviation"). The table differences of 1–3 pp therefore carry no reported uncertainty.
- For cluster C, the paper explicitly treats plain GRPO as consistent with "standard RL primarily re-weights existing solution paths" (4.3). Its boundary expansion comes from injecting teacher information in context, not from RL alone.
