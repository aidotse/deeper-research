## Cells

- `method name`: corrected — n/a: the paper names no method; it studies a phenomenon, "obfuscation generalisation", and
  adds a medical-sycophancy hold-out dataset — "We show that obfuscation can generalise across tasks" (L10)
- `method summary`: corrected — Qwen3-4B and 8B (LoRA) are trained with GRPO on modified reward-hacking multiple-choice
  datasets in which leaked information is rewarded, with a penalty from an LLM monitor (Kimi-K2) on either the CoT or the
  user-facing summary between the CoT and the answer; generalisation is tested leave-one-out across four datasets, on a
  held-out medical-sycophancy set, and on unformatted prompts. The penalty in the "output-only" condition is on the
  summary text, not on the final action: the answer itself is rewarded. — "it is also penalised for either (i) reasoning
  about the leaked information in its CoT (CoT pressure against reward hacking); or (ii) revealing its use of the hidden
  information in its response to the user (output supervision)" (L26); "the model response summary, ranging from the
  closing </think> tag to the opening <answer> tag" (L84)
- `datasets / models`: corrected — four modified reward-hacking datasets (revealed score on MMLU, factual sycophancy,
  code selection, world-affecting reward), a new medical-sycophancy hold-out, MMLU as a regulariser; Qwen3-4B and
  Qwen3-8B; Kimi-K2 / K2.5 as the monitor — "Throughout this work, we use both Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B as our initial
  policies" (L88); "We used Kimi-K2 Instruct through the Together AI API for our penalisation model" (L88)
- `metric`: confirmed, made precise — reward-hacking rate, CoT-monitor detection rate and summary-monitor detection rate
  on held-out datasets over training — "We track three metrics: (i) reward hacking rate, (ii) CoT monitor detection
  rate, and (iii) summary monitor detection rate." (L96)
- `result`: corrected — an existence result, shown in example runs, not a rate: under CoT or summary penalties, reward
  hacking rises on held-out tasks while CoT detection falls; summary penalisation obfuscates the CoT in distribution in
  "the narrow majority" of model–dataset pairs; obfuscation transferred to unformatted prompts in 3 of 4 CoT-penalty runs.
  48 runs, 4 seeds per condition, no error bars by design. — "The paper is designed as a claim that a particular
  phenomenon can happen, not about how often it happens." (checklist, item 7); "Summary penalisation generalises to

- `measures`: confirmed — monitorability — "We track three metrics: (i) reward hacking rate, (ii) CoT monitor detection

## Limitations and conditions

- "we only study this phenomenon on a single model family and two model sizes" (L140)
- "makes for an overall lack of realism" (L142); "our results should not be taken as representative example of how
  obfuscation could occur in a production RL environment" (L142)
- "Emmons et al. (2025) show that obfuscation is less frequent in harder tasks that necessitate CoT" (L144) — the tasks
  are non-load-bearing for the CoT
- "The paper is designed as a claim that a particular phenomenon can happen, not about how often it happens." (checklist
  item 7) — no frequencies or error bars
- "We note two examples where the model learns to obfuscate reasoning about reward hacking without any penalisation"
  (L196) — obfuscation also arose with no pressure at all, in 2 runs
- "the absolute detection rate may be a noisy signal in either direction" (L469) — the monitor flags degenerate CoTs
  falsely
- "We exclude data points with parsing errors $>$ 30% in our core analysis." (L220); failed runs are excluded from
  aggregation (L186)
- "equivalent OOF behaviour under summary penalisation is left to future work" (L455) — the format test covers
  CoT-penalty runs only
- "changed to Kimi-K2.5 when the latter was deprecated for the Qwen3-8B runs" (L88) — the monitor differs between scales
- "we opted to increase its saliency for Qwen3 4B" (L310) — the prompt and XML tags are contrived for small models

## Other findings

- The draft's "only the final action is [penalised]" is wrong: the summary is penalised, and the action (the answer) is
  rewarded. "Output supervision" here means the summary text.
- The draft's dimension `pressure` is right for the headline result, but the paper has a second, monitor-in-reward
  condition.
